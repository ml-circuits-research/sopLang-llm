# Proposal: New SOP Lang wire types for the compiled-plan circuits

This document is an evidence-backed proposal for adding new wire commands to SOP Lang. It is the output of a read-only investigation; no source, spec, training data, or test file was modified, and the only write is this file. Every count below was reproduced from the files named, and every percentage is given with its count. The owner's acceptance criterion is applied literally throughout: a candidate wire type is proposed only where it reduces lines of generated code and reduces the class of mistakes the student makes (parse errors, execution errors, guard failures), or makes those mistakes impossible; a candidate whose evidence is weak is reported as weak rather than padded.

## As measured today

The wire commands that exist are nine, declared in the standard vocabulary of `docs/specs/DS004-wire-types.md` and registered in `wires/standard/index.mjs`: `input`, `literal`, `jsEval`, `modelCall`, `container`, `containerAdd`, `containerUpsert`, `containerRemove`, and `containerFilter`. Of these, only `literal` and `jsEval` produce a value from declared dependencies without a side effect; `containerFilter` produces a derived view, the `container*` commands stage state effects, `modelCall` is an explicit neural effect, and `input` is a binding. The value-producing computation surface is therefore exactly `literal` plus `jsEval`. The family validator in `teacher/procedural/index.mjs` enforces this at load time with the literal gate `if (!["jsEval", "literal"].includes(wire.command)) throw ...`, so an intermediate wire in a procedural family cannot today be anything else, and `docs/specs/DS008-training-data.md` (Additional circuit shapes) states the same allowed set (a literal, a jsEval, or a container command).

The shipped circuits were counted directly. `training-data/` holds 9,760 `solution.sop` files. Across them there are 9,870 `literal` wires (9,760 `slots` literals plus 110 `facts` literals) and 10,360 `jsEval` wires (9,760 `answer` wires plus 600 intermediate jsEval wires). The wire count per circuit is 2 wires for 9,330 circuits, 3 wires for 150 circuits, and 4 wires for 280 circuits, so 430 circuits (4.4 percent of 9,760) are multi-wire plans and the other 95.6 percent are the canonical `slots`-plus-`answer` shape. The book family modules under `teacher/families/` contribute 772 compute bodies (480 `compute:` forms and 322 shared `COMPUTE` forms), and the procedural source under `teacher/procedural/` contributes another 47 bodies (30 `compute` bodies and 17 intermediate `wires` bodies); the extraction reproduced `evaluation/census.mjs`, which scans 771 of those book bodies over 941 plans.

The recurring jsEval body shapes, counted by presence across the 819 extracted bodies, are: count/length 457 of 819 (55.8 percent), string operations (split/join/replace/slice/includes) 226 (27.6 percent), divisibility/remainder 146 (17.8 percent), money (price/cost/discount/change) 122 (14.9 percent), filter 110 (13.4 percent), map 101 (12.3 percent), time arithmetic (hours/minutes/elapsed/duration/clock) 87 (10.6 percent), geometry (area/perimeter/angle/side) 84 (10.3 percent), graph traversal (neighbour/edge/path/route/adjacency/queue) 68 (8.3 percent), set operations (new Set/unique/every/some) 67 (8.2 percent), probability 44 (5.4 percent), max/min 39 (4.8 percent), sort/rank 33 (4.0 percent), percentage 32 (3.9 percent), reduce 31 (3.8 percent), running balance 10 (1.2 percent), and per-unit scale 9 (1.1 percent). A body may carry more than one shape, so these overlap. The census of the book compute bodies alone gives the same ranking at a different denominator: stringOps 477, listOps (filter/map/reduce/indexOf/max/min) 202, divisibility 142, setOps 139, money 115, timeArithmetic 85, sorting 81, geometry 80, graphTraversal 64, ratio 57, percentage 44, probability 24, balanceLedger 17, over 771 bodies.

Body length is short in the center and heavy in the tail. Across the 772 book compute bodies the line count is median 8, mean 10.2, p90 21, and maximum 67, with a histogram of 476 bodies at 0 to 9 lines, 202 at 10 to 19, 61 at 20 to 29, 23 at 30 to 39, and 10 at 40 or more. The longest bodies are the world-as-a-system meta-reasoning and navigation families (67, 65, 57, 55, and 53 lines) and the scientific-reasoning forms (52 and 48 lines), which are exactly the bodies that mix map-scale geometry, clock arithmetic, and graph/navigation traversal.

The operator inventory that generates the procedural composition circuits (`teacher/procedural/compositions.mjs`) gives an exact shape-to-line map, which is the cleanest place to measure line cost. Twenty-one of the twenty-three operators transcribe to a single jsEval line (keepAbove, keepBelow, total, count, largest, smallest, double, perUnit, addRate, subtractRate, keepDivisibleBy, modulo, ratioPer, percentOf, discount, nthLargest, uniqueCount, squareArea, elapsed, rectangleArea, neighbourCount). Two do not: `probability` transcribes to 8 lines (a filter, a length, Euclid's gcd in a while loop, and a reduced-fraction rendering) and `pathExists` transcribes to 16 lines (an adjacency map build, a BFS queue with a seen set, and a yes/no verdict). The full composition body adds a 4-line preamble, one comment and one assignment per stage, and a probe plus a return, so a depth-2 composition such as keepAbove-then-total is 12 lines, the probability composition above-divisible-probability is 22 lines, and the path composition above-largest-path-exists is 30 lines. The inventory declares 50 compositions (39 trained, 11 held out).

The failure classes were tabulated from `evaluation/registry/exp-012-census/items/holdout.jsonl` and `evaluation/registry/exp-013-1.5b/items/holdout.jsonl`, 585 items each, using the seven-class ladder of `evaluation/run-eval.mjs` (generation_transport_error, wrapper_rejected, parse_invalid, graph_invalid, execution_error, answer_mismatch, answer_match). Neither run produced a single wrapper_rejected, parse_invalid, or graph_invalid item: every completion extracted, parsed, and graph-validated. exp-012 scores 258 answer_match, 283 execution_error, and 44 answer_mismatch; exp-013 scores 322 answer_match, 162 execution_error, and 101 answer_mismatch. All failure therefore sits in execution_error and answer_mismatch, and the execution_error detail field carries the probe text that fired.

The execution_error detail decomposes into four recurring classes, and the two runs agree on the ranking. Compile errors inside the jsEval body dominate the single largest bucket: exp-012 has 70 `missing ) after argument list` and 4 `Unexpected token ';'` plus single-token variants, and exp-013 has 38 `Unexpected token ')'`, 4 `missing ) after argument list`, 2 `Unexpected token ';'`, and one each of `Unexpected token '&&'`, `Identifier 'minutes' has already been declared`, and `Identifier 'times' has already been declared`; compile errors are roughly 28 percent of execution errors in both arms (about 80 of 283 in exp-012 and about 46 of 162 in exp-013). Guard failures are the second bucket: exp-012 has 64 `the answer must be a whole number that is not negative`, 10 `the consumed days must not exceed the delivery day count`, and 6 `the case must state a closing time of the form HH:MM`; exp-013 has 14 `the plan must complete every task`. Runtime type errors are the third: exp-012 has 7 `discriminating.every is not a function`, 4 `slots.groups.reduce is not a function`, and 4 `canStart.map is not a function`; exp-013 has 11 `ready.after is not iterable`, 3 `completed.last is not a function`, and 4 `Cannot read properties of undefined`. A small fourth bucket is variable reuse and missing helpers: exp-013 has 5 `Assignment to constant variable` and 5 `formatTime is not defined`, and both runs have a few `budget_exceeded` items.

Two correlations between body shape and failure are exact rather than anecdotal. First, all 64 `whole number that is not negative` failures in exp-012 come from exactly two held-out census compositions: `above-total-percent-discount` (33) and `above-third-largest-percent` (31), which is the percentage/discount/rank operator group producing a fractional or negative result that trips the whole-number guard. Second, all 25 items that fail with `ready.after is not iterable` or `the plan must complete every task` in exp-013 are in the decompose-to-solve book, which is the task-dependency and scheduling family — the graph-shaped bodies; decompose-to-solve is also the single largest execution-error book in exp-013 at 83 items.

The prior finding D-G in `evaluation/registry/phase4-analysis.md` records four coupled gates that block the container and registry-reading shapes of DS008: the family validator accepts only jsEval and literal as intermediate wires (`teacher/procedural/index.mjs`), the program builder has no container wire path (`teacher/families/index.mjs` buildProgram), the provenance battery judges reactivity from slots/facts references in the answer wire (`training-data/provenance.mjs`), and no manifest column records the structural read set a definition-reading plan must publish. D-G is evidence that the pipeline is jsEval-and-literal-only today and that shapes beyond a single jsEval body are already part of the spec but unimplemented, so a new wire command is a parser-and-runtime change plus a dataset migration, not a free addition. DS008 fixes the target-form rules any new wire must respect: a dataset circuit carries a `slots` literal, an optional `facts` literal, and a deterministic `answer` wire, with no `input` and no `modelCall`; intermediate wires may be literal, jsEval, or container commands; every wire is subject to its command's input and output contract; the plan fingerprint covers every wire's definition body; and the provenance battery still requires the answer to react to perturbed slots. DS009 fixes the measurement rules: seven outcome classes in ladder order, oracle match as the primary metric with parse/graph/completion reported separately, and arm comparison over the same suite and seeds with per-item records before aggregates.

One further fact is load-bearing for the migration-cost argument. The `jsEval` command has already moved generic text out of the model's body twice: version 2 asserts the input and output contract itself (defined dependencies, non-empty slots record, non-null non-undefined non-empty result), and version 2.1 provides the `probe(condition, message)` helper in the sandbox. `wires/standard/jsEval.mjs` records that the fixed probe preamble was 18.96 percent of the target tokens of the shipped suite before this removal. A new wire type is the same move applied to one specific operation instead of the generic contract: the operation's transcription and its guard leave the body and become the command.

## Candidate wire types

Each candidate names the command, its semantics, its input/output contract, the measured pattern it replaces, the measured line reduction, the error class it eliminates and the evidence, the risks, and the required migration. All candidates share the same migration skeleton and every one states it. A new standard command means: a new `wires/standard/<name>.mjs` module with a manifest, analyzer, validator, and executor per DS004; a version increment on the standard vocabulary and a `DS002-sop-lang-core.md` edit to the vocabulary list, a `DS004-wire-types.md` edit to the command table, and a `DS008-training-data.md` edit if the shape enters the dataset contract; a runtime version increment recorded in the trace and cache keys (the registry already case-insensitively accepts any command, so `runtime/parser.mjs` needs no grammar change, but the command registry, the capability catalog, and the command implementation hash all move); and a full dataset regeneration, because the teacher families that emit the operation must now emit the new wire, the export re-runs, and every downstream plan fingerprint, verification gate, and manifest hash changes. The parser accepts an arbitrary command token by design, so parser changes are limited to any analyzer or validator the new command carries, not to the declaration grammar.

### 1. graphPath — graph reachability and neighbour query (highest evidence)

Name and semantics. `graphPath` answers a reachability or adjacency question over a stated undirected edge list without writing a traversal. Its body names a source list of edges, a start node, and either a target node (reachability, returning `yes` or `no`) or a neighbour-count request (returning the degree). The runtime builds the adjacency structure, runs the bounded traversal in the host implementation, and returns the verdict, so the model never writes a queue, a seen set, or a shift loop.

Input/output contract. Body form is declarative, for example an edge list plus `from` and `to` fields or a `count` flag; dependencies are the declared `$wire` values named in the body fields; the analyzer reads exactly those references. The executor validates that the edges are pairs of comparable scalars, that `from` appears in at least one edge, and (for reachability) that `from` and `to` are distinct nodes that both appear; a violation is a compilation or validation failure before execution. Output is a whole number for a neighbour count or the strings `yes`/`no` for reachability, and the command owns the whole-number and yes/no contract, which is the same contract the generated body today re-asserts with its own probe.

Measured pattern it replaces. It replaces the `pathExists` operator transcription (16 jsEval lines: adjacency Map build, BFS queue, seen set, yes/no verdict) and the `neighbourCount` operator transcription (1 line but error-prone reduce over edges), and it is the natural target for the book families' dependency and navigation bodies. In the inventory these operators appear in 4 compositions; in the census, graphTraversal is 64 of 771 book bodies (8.3 percent) and my body measurement finds graph language in 68 of 819 bodies (8.3 percent).

Measured line reduction. The 16-line pathExists transcription collapses to one declarative body, a reduction of 15 lines per occurrence; the full above-largest-path-exists composition falls from 30 lines to 15. For the book dependency bodies, the reduction is larger in absolute terms because those bodies reach 46 to 67 lines and the reachability core is a large fraction of them, though the exact saving per body is not a constant.

Error class eliminated and the evidence. The decompose-to-solve dependency and schedule families are the largest execution-error book in exp-013 (83 items), and 25 of those are the two named graph failures `ready.after is not iterable` (11) and `the plan must complete every task` (14); the same book also produces `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'after')` and `completed.last is not a function`. A declarative graph command removes the queue/iteration and the free-form adjacency code that produces "is not iterable" and "is not a function", and it moves the reachability correctness onto the runtime, so the class of graph-execution errors is not reduced but made impossible for the operation it covers. This is the only candidate whose line reduction (15 to 16 lines) and its named error class are both large.

Risks and open questions. The book bodies are task-dependency scheduling and navigation, not always literal reachability, so a single `graphPath` command covers the core but not every scheduling variant (deadlines, parallel groups, buffers); the command may need a small family (neighbour count, reachability, and a successor list) rather than one verb. The operator exists in the inventory but no family has been generated into `training-data/` yet, so the frequency claim is prospective on the graph side and current only on the book side. The traversal must be bounded and deterministic, which is a runtime implementation cost.

### 2. fraction — probability as a reduced fraction (strong line reduction, weak frequency)

Name and semantics. `fraction` reduces a ratio to a reduced fraction or a whole number: it takes a favourable count and a total and returns `numerator/denominator` or the whole integer, running Euclid's gcd in the command. The body names the favourable count and the total (or the list and a divisor predicate).

Input/output contract. Body carries the numerator and denominator sources (or a source list and a divisibility divisor); the analyzer reads the named `$wire` references. The validator rejects a zero denominator and a favourable count outside 0 to total. Output is the string form `a/b` or the whole number `a`, exactly the reduced form the generated body computes today with an 8-line transcription and re-asserts with a regex probe.

Measured pattern it replaces. The `probability` operator transcription: 8 lines — a filter, a length, a five-statement Euclid while loop, and the numerator/denominator rendering — plus the probe `typeof current === "string" && /^\d+(\/\d+)?$/`.

Measured line reduction. 8 lines to 1 declarative body, a reduction of 7 lines per occurrence; the above-divisible-probability composition falls from 22 to 15 lines.

Error class eliminated and the evidence. The command removes the gcd loop (a place where off-by-one arithmetic and unreduced fractions live), division by zero, and integer-versus-fraction rendering. The failure data is thin here: probability is 24 of 771 census bodies (2.5 percent) and 44 of 819 measured bodies (5.4 percent), and no execution_error in exp-012 or exp-013 is a probability probe, because the shipped suite barely contains probability circuits. This candidate is supported for line reduction but under-supported for error frequency, and it must be ranked behind graphPath.

Risks and open questions. Low frequency means the measured mistake reduction is unproven; the wire earns its place only if the next tranche of procedural families adds probability compositions at a learnable dose, which is the census plan but is not yet generated.

### 3. percent — percentage and discount with a whole-number contract (strong error evidence, weak line reduction)

Name and semantics. `percent` computes a percentage or a discount of a stated value under an explicit output contract (whole number, or a declared rounding rule), so a fractional result is a validation failure rather than a probe failure at run time.

Input/output contract. Body names the base value and the percentage, and whether it is a take-percent or a discount; the validator applies the stated output contract (for the current families: the result must be a positive whole number). Output is the computed amount, with the contract enforced by the command.

Measured pattern it replaces. The `percentOf` and `discount` operators (1 line each: `(current * slots.pct) / 100` and `current - (current * slots.pct) / 100`).

Measured line reduction. One line to one declarative body, so the line reduction is zero on the transcription axis; the saving is only the guard the command now owns instead of the body restating it.

Error class eliminated and the evidence. This is the most precisely grounded error elimination in the data. All 64 `the answer must be a whole number that is not negative` failures in exp-012 (22.6 percent of that run's 283 execution errors) come from exactly two held-out compositions, `above-total-percent-discount` (33) and `above-third-largest-percent` (31), and the failing operator is the percent/discount group producing a fractional or negative value. A `percent` command with a whole-number output contract turns those 64 run-time guard failures into either a validation rejection the model can fix or an enforced integer result, and the failures stop being execution errors. This candidate meets the error half of the owner's criterion but not the line half, and it must be stated that way.

Risks and open questions. Because the transcription is already one line, a new wire here is close to the "prettify" boundary the owner ruled out; the argument rests on the contract enforcement, not on code size. A rounding rule (truncate, round half up) is a semantic decision that the current families avoid by resampling, so the command must state which rule it enforces.

### 4. clock — time and clock arithmetic with formatting (strong error evidence and frequency, weak line reduction)

Name and semantics. `clock` performs the time and clock operations the book families hand-write: converting a duration between hours, minutes, and days by a stated factor, comparing two clock times, and formatting a result as `HH:MM`, with the factor stated in the body rather than assumed (which keeps it a no-knowledge operation).

Input/output contract. Body names a value, a from-unit, a to-unit, and a factor (or a comparison of two clock strings and a format); the analyzer reads the named references. The validator rejects a missing or mismatched unit and a malformed clock string. Output is a formatted string or a whole number with the unit attached, with the format enforced by the command.

Measured pattern it replaces. The `elapsed` operator (1 line) and, more importantly, the book families' hand-written clock bodies: the adult-reasoning opening-hours family (`a-public-counters-opening-hours`) and the world-as-a-system clock-arithmetic bodies.

Measured line reduction. One line on the operator axis, so weak; the book bodies are longer but the exact per-body saving is not a constant.

Error class eliminated and the evidence. The time error class is prominent in the failure data: exp-013 has 5 `formatTime is not defined`, 5 `Assignment to constant variable`, and `Identifier 'minutes' has already been declared`; exp-012 has 6 `the case must state a closing time of the form HH:MM` and 5 `the daily consumption must be a positive whole number` (with the companion `the consumed days must not exceed the delivery day count`, 10). Time arithmetic is 85 of 771 census bodies (10.6 percent) and 87 of 819 measured bodies (10.6 percent), so the frequency is among the highest of the operation groups. A `clock` command removes the free-form time formatting and the unit-variable redeclaration that produces "formatTime is not defined", "minutes has already been declared", and "Assignment to constant variable", and it enforces the HH:MM format the book families assert. This candidate meets the error half with strong frequency but not the line half, and it must be stated that way.

Risks and open questions. Time conventions (60 minutes per hour, 24 hours per day) are external knowledge; the current no-knowledge families state the factor, and the command must keep the factor in the body or file the operation under the knowledge category per DS008. A single `clock` verb risks becoming a grab-bag, so a split into a convert verb and a format verb is likely.

### 5. aggregate — filter then summarize as one declarative wire (strong frequency, moderate line reduction)

Name and semantics. `aggregate` filters a source list by a declared predicate and reduces the kept records in one command: sum, count, min, max, or average, with the predicate and the aggregate named in the body.

Input/output contract. Body names a source list, a field or comparison, and the aggregate; the analyzer reads the named references. The validator rejects an empty kept list when the aggregate needs it. Output is the scalar. The command owns the non-empty and whole-number contract the generated stages re-assert today.

Measured pattern it replaces. The filter-then-sum and filter-then-count pairs, which today are either two intermediate jsEval wires (the `qualifying` and `summary` wires of the average-of-qualifying family, each with its own probes) or a two-stage single body (`values.filter(...)` then `current.reduce(...)`).

Measured line reduction. A two-wire filter-plus-summary plan collapses to one declarative wire, saving one wire declaration, one probe preamble, and the intermediate name, and a two-stage single body drops one stage; the per-occurrence saving is a wire and its probe rather than a large line count.

Error class eliminated and the evidence. The list-method type errors are exactly the class this removes: exp-012 has `discriminating.every is not a function` (7), `slots.groups.reduce is not a function` (4), and `canStart.map is not a function` (4), and exp-013 has `completed.last is not a function` (3) and `ready.after is not iterable` (11). List operations are 202 of 771 census bodies (the second-highest group), and filter (110) plus reduce (31) plus max/min (39) are the most common computational shapes in the measured bodies. The diag-009 finding D-L records that 60 of 60 normal-condition programs declared the same two jsEval wires with an intermediate wire always named `kept`, so the filter-then-summarize pattern is the single most-taught shape. A declarative aggregate removes the method call entirely, so "X is not a function" and "X is not iterable" become impossible for the operation it covers. This candidate is supported on frequency and on error elimination, with moderate line reduction.

Risks and open questions. The reduction is structural (one wire instead of two) rather than textual, so the line-count evidence is weaker than graphPath or fraction; the predicate language has to be kept small or it becomes a second JavaScript, which would reproduce the very error class it removes.

### 6. rank — select the nth largest or smallest (weak evidence)

Name and semantics. `rank` returns the kth largest or smallest element of a source list, with the rank and direction declared in the body.

Input/output contract. Body names a source list, a direction, and a rank; the validator rejects a rank beyond the list length. Output is the scalar.

Measured pattern it replaces. The `nthLargest` operator (1 line: a copy, a sort, and an index) and the book families' sort-then-index bodies.

Measured line reduction. One line to one declarative body, so zero on the transcription axis; sorting/ranking is 81 of 771 census bodies (9.8 percent).

Error class eliminated and the evidence. The direction-pair diagnostic (diag-pairs-010) found zero of eight direction pairs correct, and the third-largest composition (`above-third-largest-percent`) is one of the two compositions behind the 64 whole-number guard failures in exp-012, but those failures are the percentage contract, not the rank transcription. The rank failure is a planning failure (choosing largest against smallest) that a wire type does not address, because a wire type changes the emission of a chosen operation, not the choice of operation. On the owner's criterion this candidate is not supported: the transcription is already one line, and the measured failure is operand selection, which is not a mistake the wire removes.

### 7. Divisibility, modulo, unique-count, and ratio (evidence does not support these)

The `keepDivisibleBy`, `modulo`, `uniqueCount`, and `ratioPer` operators are each a single jsEval line (`value % divisor`, `new Set(value).size`, `value / divisor`), and divisibility is the third-most-frequent shape (146 of 819 bodies, 17.8 percent; 142 of 771 census bodies). Their transcription is one line, so a new wire type for any of them reduces no generated code; it only renames a one-line expression into a declarative body. The guard failures they produce are already owned by the generic jsEval contract or by the family's own probe, and no execution_error in the two runs names a divisibility or set probe. Under the owner's criterion these are prettification, and the evidence does not support proposing them now. They should instead be added to the composition inventory as operators (which is already the plan) and reconsidered only if a future tranche shows a division or set error class that a contract-enforcing command could remove.

## How to decide

The deciding experiment is a controlled arm that adds the candidate wire type to the dataset and measures the mistake class it claims to remove, against the jsEval-only baseline, holding the recipe, the suite, and the split fixed. Concretely, for the top candidate `graphPath`: author procedural families that emit `graphPath` in place of the 16-line pathExists and the neighbour-count transcription, regenerate the export under the existing `compiled-plan-chat` profile with the new wire named in the system prompt (as DS008 requires for any shape change), and train one arm with exactly the exp-012/exp-013 recipe (3 epochs, learning rate 1e-4, per-device batch 4 with gradient accumulation 8, gradient checkpointing, preservation-10, save-steps 90), on the same base model and the same 9,175-row suite plus the new families. The baseline is the recorded exp-012 (0.5B) and exp-013 (1.5B) holdout on the same 585 items. The measured outcomes, all read from per-item records, are: the holdout oracle match; the execution_error count broken into the four detail classes (compile, guard, runtime-type, budget); the count of the specific failures the wire claims to remove (for graphPath, `ready.after is not iterable`, `the plan must complete every task`, and the `is not a function` family on the decompose-to-solve slice); and the mean target token count, which is the line-reduction proxy. The candidate is accepted only if the arm lowers the targeted execution_error class on the held-out compositions that use the wire without lowering oracle match elsewhere, and the target-token count falls by the measured line reduction. The same protocol applies to each candidate with its own claimed error class (for `percent`, the 64 whole-number guard failures on the two percent compositions; for `clock`, the formatTime and HH:MM failures). Because a wire type changes the dataset rather than the model alone, the baseline and the arm must differ only in the wire's presence, which requires the teacher families to emit the new wire and the held-out compositions to be generated both ways; the experiment is therefore a dataset-regeneration experiment as much as a training experiment, and the migration steps of each candidate are the entry ticket, not an afterthought.

## Ranked recommendation

Ranked by evidence, not elegance, and applying the owner's criterion of both line reduction and mistake reduction: (1) `graphPath` is the strongest proposal, the only one with a large measured line reduction (15 to 16 lines) and a large named execution-error class (the decompose-to-solve graph failures, the largest execution-error book in exp-013) that the command makes impossible for the operation it covers; (2) `fraction` has the strongest single-operation line reduction (7 lines) but the weakest frequency evidence, so it is supported for line reduction and unproven for mistake reduction; (3) `percent` has the single most precisely grounded mistake reduction (all 64 whole-number guard failures in exp-012 trace to two percent compositions) but zero line reduction, so it meets one half of the criterion and must be weighed against the owner's anti-prettify rule; (4) `clock` has strong and frequent error evidence (formatTime, HH:MM, constant reassignment across 85-plus bodies) but weak line reduction, the same one-half situation as `percent`; (5) `aggregate` has the strongest frequency evidence and a real structural reduction (two wires to one), but the reduction is structural rather than textual; (6) `rank` is not supported because its failure is operand selection, which a wire type does not change; and (7) divisibility, modulo, unique-count, and ratio are not supported and are prettification, because their transcription is already a single line and no measured failure names them. The honest summary is that only graphPath currently clears the owner's bar on both axes, that fraction clears it on lines but not on frequency, and that the percent, clock, and aggregate candidates clear it on mistakes but not on lines; the divisibility, set, and ratio candidates should not be proposed. Nothing in this investigation was modified; the only file written is this one.
