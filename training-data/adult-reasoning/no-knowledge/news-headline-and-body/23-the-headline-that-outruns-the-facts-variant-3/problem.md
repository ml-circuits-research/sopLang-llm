# 23 — The headline that outruns the facts — variant 3

A site publishes the headline “CHAOS at the market in Wells Village: tomato prices EXPLODE”. Deck: “Sellers ask 94 per kilo”. Body, signed Piotr:

“Tuesday morning, at three stalls out of five, field tomatoes were 9 a kilo. At one stall a 400 g ‘tasting edition’ bowl was ticketed at 94 a kilo. At the last stall: 11 a kilo. A shopper said last week they were 8. There is no official register in the visit. The town hall, after the edition closed: ‘we have received no written complaints’. Opening photo: a 9-a-kilo ticket.”

Comment 1: “So every kilo is 94; the headline says so.” Comment 2: “The article proves the town hall is lying.”

Question. What does the body support, what is only a headline, and which comment follows from the text?
