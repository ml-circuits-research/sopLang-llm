# Explanation 29 — The headline that outruns the facts — variant 9

## Explanation

1. The body reports, for the market in Long Hill, 3 of the 5 stalls on one morning, with field tomatoes between 9 and 11 a kilo, so the supported claim is a small sample and a range, not a market-wide price.
2. The one price the deck shouts about is the 400 g “tasting edition” bowl at 136 a kilo, a single item Ugo describes beside the range, so the headline generalises what the body shows once.
3. The first comment repeats that deck price as if every kilo cost 136, and the second turns the town hall's “we have received no written complaints” into a lie about prices, which the body never states; the shopper's 8 a kilo was a recollection of last week, not a price of the visit.
4. Nothing in the body supports either comment, so the answer is that neither follows the text.

Reference material as printed in the source:

Three layers: headline, reporter’s facts, readers’ inferences.

The facts have place, time, and a stall count. The figure is tied to 400 g of tasting stock, not to the field kilo. The town hall speaks about complaints, not about prices — so it is not caught in a lie about prices.

Rule: a headline is an invitation. The premise is the checkable sentence. When the body is poorer than the headline, stay with the body. “Everyone” and “proves they lie” are leaps.
