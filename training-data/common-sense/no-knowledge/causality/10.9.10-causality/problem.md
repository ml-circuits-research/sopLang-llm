# 10.9.10 — Causality

Over four weeks, days using procedure X also show better outcomes. However, X is used mainly when staffing is complete. To separate the effects, the next week randomly assigns 40 comparable cases: 20 receive X and 20 receive the usual procedure under the same team and stated conditions. In the X group, 15 of 20 reach the target; in the control group, 11 of 20 do so. The model states no other known differences.

Question. Which evidence is merely correlational and which provides a stronger causal argument? Can absolute causal certainty be claimed? Explain the role of the confounder 'complete staffing' and of random assignment.
