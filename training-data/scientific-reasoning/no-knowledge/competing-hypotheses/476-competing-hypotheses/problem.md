# 476 — Competing hypotheses

Problem world. In the model, Earth is approximated as a sphere rotating around its axis. The side facing the Sun is illuminated, while the opposite side is in night. As Earth rotates, a place can move from the illuminated region to the dark region and back again. These problems do not require astronomical distances or real durations; we use only the stated geometric model.

Case data. Hypothesis 1: H1: alternation comes from rotation of the sphere → predictions [the point marked enters and goes out from light, rotation changes the state, the source remains fixed]. Hypothesis 2: H2: alternation comes from turning off the source → predictions [rotation is not necessary, the source turns off, all the points darkens simultaneously]. In the experiment, we observe “the point marked enters and goes out from light”.

Question. Which hypothesis is better supported, and which is eliminated by this observation in the model? State the conclusion without saying more than the data allow.
