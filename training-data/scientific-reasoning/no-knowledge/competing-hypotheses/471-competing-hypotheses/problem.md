# 471 — Competing hypotheses

Problem world. In the water-cycle model, liquid water can evaporate, water vapor can cool and condense into droplets, and sufficiently large droplets can fall as precipitation. Fallen water can collect in rivers, lakes, or soil and later re-enter the cycle. Clouds are not explained as “water smoke,” but as very small droplets formed after cooling and condensation.

Case data. Hypothesis 1: H1: there is too little water vapor → predictions [low evaporation, little water vapor, heating increases the droplets]. Hypothesis 2: H2: the water vapor does not cool → predictions [much water vapor, few droplets, cooling increases condensation]. In the experiment, we observe “low evaporation”.

Question. Which hypothesis is better supported, and which is eliminated by this observation in the model? State the conclusion without saying more than the data allow.
