# 481 — Competing hypotheses

Problem world. In our model, rock fragments can be broken off by weathering, transported by water or wind, and then deposited when the transporting agent loses energy. Repeated deposits can form layers. Traces or remains of organisms can become trapped in sediments and, under certain conditions, become fossils. A fossil does not form instantly; the problem uses only the logical order of the stages, not their real duration.

Case data. Hypothesis 1: H1: the lack of the layer comes from transport that is too strong → predictions [the water is fast-flowing, little deposition, the sediments appear farther downstream]. Hypothesis 2: H2: the lack of the layer comes from a lack of fragments → predictions [the water may be slow-flowing, no deposits appear in the collector, no fragments are present]. In the experiment, we observe “the water is fast-flowing”.

Question. Which hypothesis is better supported, and which is eliminated by this observation in the model? State the conclusion without saying more than the data allow.
