# 386 — Competing hypotheses

Problem world. In the small meadow described here, grass is eaten by rabbits, and rabbits can be eaten by foxes. If the food available to one group decreases greatly, that group has fewer resources; the effect can then continue along the chain. We will not assume that animal numbers change instantly: we track only the likely direction of the effect after some time.

Case data. Hypothesis 1: H1: rabbits decrease from lack of food → predictions [little grass, rabbits decrease, the tracks of fox must not increase]. Hypothesis 2: H2: rabbits decrease through predation → predictions [grass sufficient, rabbits decrease, the tracks of fox increase]. In the experiment, we observe “little grass”.

Question. Which hypothesis is better supported, and which is eliminated by this observation in the model? State the conclusion without saying more than the data allow.
