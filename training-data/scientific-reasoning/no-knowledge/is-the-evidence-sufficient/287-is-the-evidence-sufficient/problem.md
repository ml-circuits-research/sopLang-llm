# 287 — Is the evidence sufficient?

Problem world. In our model, a sensory receptor detects a stimulus, sends a nerve signal, the control center interprets the information, and muscles can produce a response. If the signal does not reach the control center, a response based on that signal cannot be chosen correctly. A fast response is not automatically a correct response; the information must first be detected and transmitted.

Case data. Two hypotheses remain possible. H1: the receiver does not detect predicts [without signal initial, reaction absent, stimulus stronger helps]. H2: the path of signal is blocked predicts [the center does not receive, signal initial there is, stimulus stronger does not help]. Until now we know only “the general problem that both hypotheses try to explain”, while the problem explicitly states that this information is compatible with both hypotheses.

Question. Is this information sufficient to choose a hypothesis? If not, what evidence would genuinely discriminate between them?
