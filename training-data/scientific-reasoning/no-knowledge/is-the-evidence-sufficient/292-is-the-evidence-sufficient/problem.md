# 292 — Is the evidence sufficient?

Problem world. In the model, microbes can transfer from a surface to the hands and then from the hands to the mouth if the hands are not cleaned. Correct washing with soap and water greatly reduces the number of microbes on the hands, without meaning that every microbe in the world disappears. To reduce risk, we can break the transmission chain at several points.

Case data. Two hypotheses remain possible. H1: transmission is through hands predicts [microbes on hands, washing reduces cases, surface is reached]. H2: transmission is direct through food predicts [covering of the food reduces cases, the food are exposed, the hands can be clean]. Until now we know only “the general problem that both hypotheses try to explain”, while the problem explicitly states that this information is compatible with both hypotheses.

Question. Is this information sufficient to choose a hypothesis? If not, what evidence would genuinely discriminate between them?
