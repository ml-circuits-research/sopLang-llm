# 257 — Is the evidence sufficient?

Problem world. In this model, the root absorbs water from the soil, the stem transports it toward the leaves, and the leaves use water, carbon dioxide, and light to make nutrients. If the water pathway through the stem is interrupted, the leaves may receive too little water even when the soil is moist. We will treat a healthy leaf as needing both transported water and light.

Case data. Two hypotheses remain possible. H1: the soil is too dry predicts [wilted leaves, dry soil, watering helps]. H2: the stem does not transport water predicts [wilted leaves, moist soil, watering alone does not help]. Until now we know only “wilted leaves”, while the problem explicitly states that this information is compatible with both hypotheses.

Question. Is this information sufficient to choose a hypothesis? If not, what evidence would genuinely discriminate between them?
