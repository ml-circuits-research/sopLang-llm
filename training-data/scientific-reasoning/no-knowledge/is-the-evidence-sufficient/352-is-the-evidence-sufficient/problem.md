# 352 — Is the evidence sufficient?

Problem world. In the model, Earth is approximated as a sphere rotating around its axis. The side facing the Sun is illuminated, while the opposite side is in night. As Earth rotates, a place can move from the illuminated region to the dark region and back again. These problems do not require astronomical distances or real durations; we use only the stated geometric model.

Case data. Two hypotheses remain possible. H1: alternation comes from rotation of the sphere predicts [the point marked enters and goes out from light, rotation changes the state, the source remains fixed]. H2: alternation comes from turning off the source predicts [rotation is not necessary, the source turns off, all the points darkens simultaneously]. Until now we know only “the general problem that both hypotheses try to explain”, while the problem explicitly states that this information is compatible with both hypotheses.

Question. Is this information sufficient to choose a hypothesis? If not, what evidence would genuinely discriminate between them?
