# 282 — Is the evidence sufficient?

Problem world. In the simplified model, inhaled air brings oxygen into the lungs; oxygen passes into the blood, and the blood carries it to the cells. Cells produce carbon dioxide, which is carried by the blood back to the lungs and then exhaled. If one transport link is blocked, stages after that link receive less of the transported substance.

Case data. Two hypotheses remain possible. H1: air not reaches sufficient in lungs predicts [little air, low blood oxygen, ventilation helps]. H2: the transfer in blood is problem predicts [normal air, low blood oxygen, ventilation alone does not solve]. Until now we know only “low blood oxygen”, while the problem explicitly states that this information is compatible with both hypotheses.

Question. Is this information sufficient to choose a hypothesis? If not, what evidence would genuinely discriminate between them?
