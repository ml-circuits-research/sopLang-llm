# 620 — Spatial relationships and orientation

Given knowledge. River water flows from upstream to downstream and can carry particles or substances. In the treatment-plant model, screening stops large objects, filtration retains smaller particles, and a separate disinfection stage is required to reduce microorganisms. A particle filter does not automatically remove all dissolved substances. A pollution source upstream can affect points downstream.

Problem data. On a grid map, a step north/south/east/west has the same length. We know: “moist zone” is south of “the upstream village”; “the water outlet” is west of “moist zone”; “the filtration station” is north of “the water outlet”; “the downstream city” is northeast of “moist zone”.

Question. Where is “the filtration station” relative to “the upstream village”? How many elementary moves are required along the described route between them? Represent the coordinates mentally or symbolically.
