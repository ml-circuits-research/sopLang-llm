# 555 — Spatial relationships and orientation

Given knowledge. Two gears that touch directly rotate in opposite directions. In the model, if a small gear drives a larger gear, the larger gear makes fewer rotations in the same time; an odd or even number of contacts can change the final direction. The teeth prevent slipping in the ideal model. An intermediate gear can change direction without being the useful output.

Problem data. On a grid map, a step north/south/east/west has the same length. We know: “the gear A” is west of “the input axle”; “the gear B” is south of “the gear A”; “the gear C” is east of “the gear B”; “the output axle” is northeast of “the gear A”.

Question. Where is “the gear C” relative to “the input axle”? How many elementary moves are required along the described route between them? Represent the coordinates mentally or symbolically.
