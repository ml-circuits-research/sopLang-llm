# 222 — Predicting a change

Problem world. In the water-cycle model, liquid water can evaporate, water vapor can cool and condense into droplets, and sufficiently large droplets can fall as precipitation. Fallen water can collect in rivers, lakes, or soil and later re-enter the cycle. Clouds are not explained as “water smoke,” but as very small droplets formed after cooling and condensation.

Case data. Before the change, the system is stable. Now we activate or increase “heating a of the water”. The causal rules are: heating a of the water → more intense evaporation. Water vapor in air → possible condensation at cooling. Condensation → droplets. Droplets large enough → precipitation.

Question. Predict the effects that may appear downstream. Separate the direct effect from the indirect effects.
