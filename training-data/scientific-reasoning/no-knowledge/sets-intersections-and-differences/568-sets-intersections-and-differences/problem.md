# 568 — Sets, intersections, and differences

Given knowledge. Air occupies space and can be compressed. In a model of a closed syringe, pushing the plunger decreases the volume of the air and increases pressure; if the air has a path out, pressure does not increase in the same way. Air is matter even though we cannot see it. A balloon can stretch when the pressure inside increases.

Problem data. We use the properties as set labels. syringe A: has an opening; the balloon B: keeps air trapped, can change its volume; the container C: keeps air trapped, has an opening; the tube D: can change its volume, has an opening; the piston E: keeps air trapped, can change its volume, has an opening.

Question. Construct A=“keeps air trapped”, B=“can change its volume”, C=“has an opening”. Calculate B∩C, A\B and A∪C. Explain for each operation what you keep and what you eliminate.
