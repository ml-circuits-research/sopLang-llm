# 553 — Sets, intersections, and differences

Given knowledge. Two gears that touch directly rotate in opposite directions. In the model, if a small gear drives a larger gear, the larger gear makes fewer rotations in the same time; an odd or even number of contacts can change the final direction. The teeth prevent slipping in the ideal model. An intermediate gear can change direction without being the useful output.

Problem data. We use the properties as set labels. The gear A: has 10 teeth, is in direct contact with A; the gear B: has 10 teeth; the gear C: is in direct contact with A, is the output gear; the gear D: has 10 teeth, is in direct contact with A, is the output gear; the gear E: is the output gear.

Question. Construct A=“has 10 teeth”, B=“is in direct contact with A”, C=“is the output gear”. Calculate B∩C, A\B and A∪C. Explain for each operation what you keep and what you eliminate.
