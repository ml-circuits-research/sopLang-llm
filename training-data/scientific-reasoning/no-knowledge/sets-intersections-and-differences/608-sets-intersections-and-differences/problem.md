# 608 — Sets, intersections, and differences

Given knowledge. In the hiking model, the map shows distances and elevation changes, weather can change speed, and the team has a limited reserve of water and energy. Layered clothing reduces heat loss under the stated cold conditions. Map scale allows map distance to be converted into real distance. Climbing uses more energy in the model than walking on level ground.

Problem data. We use the properties as set labels. The route A: is short, remains open during rain; the route B: has a small elevation gain; the route C: is short, has a small elevation gain; the route D: has a small elevation gain, remains open during rain; the route E: is short, has a small elevation gain, remains open during rain.

Question. Construct A=“is short”, B=“has a small elevation gain”, C=“remains open during rain”. Calculate A∩B, A∩C and C\A. Explain for each operation what you keep and what you eliminate.
