# 563 — Sets, intersections, and differences

Given knowledge. For equal volumes, the material with smaller mass has lower density. In the model, an object floats in water if its average density is lower than the density of water; a hollow shape can contain air and reduce average density. A compact piece of metal can be denser than water. A metal ship can float because its total volume contains a great deal of air.

Problem data. We use the properties as set labels. The object A: has an air cavity; the object B: has an air cavity, has a large volume; the object C: has a large volume; the object D: has a large volume, has low mass for its volume; the object E: has an air cavity, has low mass for its volume.

Question. Construct A=“has an air cavity”, B=“has a large volume”, C=“has low mass for its volume”. Calculate A∩B, A∩C and C\A. Explain for each operation what you keep and what you eliminate.
