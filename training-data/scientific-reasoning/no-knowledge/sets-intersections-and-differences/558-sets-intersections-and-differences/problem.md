# 558 — Sets, intersections, and differences

Given knowledge. An object remains stable in the model if the projection of its center of mass stays above its base of support. A wider base or moving mass inward can increase stability. In the model, the center of mass is the point at which we can treat mass as concentrated for balance analysis. Raising mass higher can make the system easier to tip.

Problem data. We use the properties as set labels. The model A: has a wide base, has the load secured; the model B: has a low mass position; the model C: has a wide base, has a low mass position; the model D: has a low mass position, has the load secured; the model E: has a wide base, has a low mass position, has the load secured.

Question. Construct A=“has a wide base”, B=“has a low mass position”, C=“has the load secured”. Calculate A∩B∩C, B\C and A∪B. Explain for each operation what you keep and what you eliminate.
