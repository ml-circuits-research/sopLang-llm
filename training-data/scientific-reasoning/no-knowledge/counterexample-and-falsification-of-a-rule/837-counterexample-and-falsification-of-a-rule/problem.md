# 837 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. In the circuit model, the metals used here are conductors, while plastic and rubber are insulators. Current appears only if there is a continuous conducting path between the source terminals; insulation is used to prevent contact with conducting parts. A conductor allows current to pass in the model. An insulator breaks the electrical path.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the wire A: is conductor; the clamp B: is conductor, is insulated on the outside; the seed coat C: is insulated on the outside; the switch D: is insulated on the outside, can close the path; the bulb E: is conductor, can close the path. A peer states: “any case with the property “can close the path” also has the property “is insulated on the outside”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
