# 782 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Incisors cut, canines can tear, and molars crush and grind. Enamel protects the outside of the tooth. In the model, preparing a piece of food well requires cutting or tearing and then grinding before swallowing. Teeth have different shapes for different mechanical roles. Chewing increases the surface area of pieces of food.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the incisor A: can cut, removes deposits; the canine B: can crush; the molar C: can cut, can crush; the toothbrush D: can crush, removes deposits; the piece E: can cut, can crush, removes deposits. A peer states: “Any case with the property “can cut” also has the property “can crush”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
