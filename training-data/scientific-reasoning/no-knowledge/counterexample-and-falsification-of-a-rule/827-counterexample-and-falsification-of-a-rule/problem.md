# 827 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. A mirror does not produce light; it changes the direction of light that reaches its surface. In the grid-ray model, the outgoing angle is symmetric with the incoming angle relative to the normal to the mirror. Light travels in straight lines in the uniform medium of the model. A matte surface scatters light in many directions.

Problem data. The table of observations is: mirror A: reflects toward right, is diagonally oriented; mirror B: reflects toward right; the screen C: is diagonally oriented, is on the route; the source D: reflects toward right, is diagonally oriented, is on the route; the obstacle E: is on the route. A peer states: “any case with the property “reflects toward right” also has the property “is on the route”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
