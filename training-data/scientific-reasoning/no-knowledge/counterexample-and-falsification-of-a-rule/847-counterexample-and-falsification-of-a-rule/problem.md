# 847 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. In the model, the Moon does not produce its own visible light; it reflects sunlight. Half of the Moon is illuminated by the Sun, and the phase seen from Earth depends on the relative positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon. Moon phases are not caused by Earth’s shadow; Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse. The Moon travels in orbit around Earth.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the position A: shows the illuminated part, is opposite the Sun, is between Earth and Sun; the position B: shows the illuminated part, is between Earth and Sun; the position C: is opposite the Sun; the position D: shows the illuminated part; the observer E: is opposite the Sun, is between Earth and Sun. A peer states: “any case with the property “is opposite the Sun” also has the property “is between Earth and Sun”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
