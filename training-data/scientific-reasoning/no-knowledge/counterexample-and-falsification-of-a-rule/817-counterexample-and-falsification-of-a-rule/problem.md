# 817 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Air occupies space and can be compressed. In a model of a closed syringe, pushing the plunger decreases the volume of the air and increases pressure; if the air has a path out, pressure does not increase in the same way. Air is matter even though we cannot see it. A balloon can stretch when the pressure inside increases.

Problem data. The table of observations is: syringe A: has an opening; the balloon B: keeps air trapped, can change its volume; the container C: keeps air trapped, has an opening; the tube D: can change its volume, has an opening; the piston E: keeps air trapped, can change its volume, has an opening. A peer states: “Any case with the property “can change its volume” also has the property “has an opening”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
