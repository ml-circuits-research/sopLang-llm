# 752 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. In the simplified model, pollination succeeds when compatible pollen reaches a flower’s stigma. A visitor can touch a flower without carrying pollen, and carrying pollen does not help if the correct part of the flower is not touched. Nectar may attract insects, but nectar is not pollen. Some plants are pollinated mainly by animals, others mainly by wind.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the bee A: carries pollen, touches the stigma; the bumblebee B: carries pollen; the butterfly C: touches the stigma, visits in the morning; fly D: carries pollen, touches the stigma, visits in the morning; the beetle E: visits in the morning. A peer states: “any case with the property “carries pollen” also has the property “touches the stigma”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
