# 812 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. For equal volumes, the material with smaller mass has lower density. In the model, an object floats in water if its average density is lower than the density of water; a hollow shape can contain air and reduce average density. A compact piece of metal can be denser than water. A metal ship can float because its total volume contains a great deal of air.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the object A: has an air cavity; the object B: has an air cavity, has a large volume; the object C: has a large volume; the object D: has a large volume, has low mass for its volume; the object E: has an air cavity, has low mass for its volume. A peer states: “Any case with the property “has an air cavity” also has the property “has a large volume”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
