# 762 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Fungi and bacteria can decompose organic remains. In the model, active decomposition needs decomposers, enough water, and a suitable temperature; the matter in the remains is transformed and some substances return to the soil. Dead leaves do not disappear instantly; decomposition has stages. Strong drying can slow the activity of many decomposers.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the leaf A: is moist; the twig B: is moist, is broken into smaller pieces; the shell C: is broken into smaller pieces; dry grass D: is broken into smaller pieces, is reached by decomposers; the remainder of fruit E: is moist, is reached by decomposers. A peer states: “any case with the property “is reached by decomposers” also has the property “is moist”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
