# 842 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Energy can be transferred and transformed. In the model, a battery stores chemical energy, a circuit transfers it electrically, a bulb produces light and heat, and a motor produces motion and heat. A device does not create energy from nothing. Useful energy depends on the purpose of the device.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the battery A: produces motion; the bulb B: stores energy, produces light; the motor C: stores energy, produces motion; the wire D: produces light, produces motion; the resistor E: stores energy, produces light, produces motion. A peer states: “Any case with the property “stores energy” also has the property “produces light”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
