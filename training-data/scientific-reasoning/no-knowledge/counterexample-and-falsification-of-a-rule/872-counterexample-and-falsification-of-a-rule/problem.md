# 872 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. In the kitchen model, heating transfers energy, dissolving forms a homogeneous mixture only for certain substances and quantities, and clean utensils reduce contamination. Experimental recipes can be compared only when quantities and times are measured in the same way. A dissolved substance has not disappeared; it is distributed through the solvent. Stirring can speed dissolving without changing the maximum amount allowed by the model.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the container A: is clean, keeps heat, allows the measurement of the temperature; the container B: is clean, allows the measurement of the temperature; the spoon C: keeps heat; the lid D: is clean; the thermometer E: keeps heat, allows the measurement of the temperature. A peer states: “any case with the property “is clean” also has the property “keeps heat”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
