# 802 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Two gears that touch directly rotate in opposite directions. In the model, if a small gear drives a larger gear, the larger gear makes fewer rotations in the same time; an odd or even number of contacts can change the final direction. The teeth prevent slipping in the ideal model. An intermediate gear can change direction without being the useful output.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the gear A: has 10 teeth, is in direct contact with A; the gear B: has 10 teeth; the gear C: is in direct contact with A, is the output gear; the gear D: has 10 teeth, is in direct contact with A, is the output gear; the gear E: is the output gear. A peer states: “any case with the property “is in direct contact with A” also has the property “has 10 teeth”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
