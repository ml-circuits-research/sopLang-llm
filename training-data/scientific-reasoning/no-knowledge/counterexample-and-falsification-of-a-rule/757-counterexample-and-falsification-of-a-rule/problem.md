# 757 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Seeds can be dispersed by wind, water, or animals. In the model, a seed reaches a new place only if it leaves the parent plant and remains capable of germinating after transport. Light seeds or seeds with wings can be carried more easily by wind. Some fruits have hooks that attach to fur.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the seed with wing A: is light in weight, can float; the fruit with hooks B: can cling; the fruit floating C: is light in weight, can cling; the acorn D: can cling, can float; the seed fluffy E: is light in weight, can cling, can float. A peer states: “Any case with the property “can cling” also has the property “can float”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
