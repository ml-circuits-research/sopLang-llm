# 822 — Counterexample and falsification of a rule

Given knowledge. Evaporation occurs at the surface of a liquid. In the model, a larger surface area, moving air, and a higher temperature can speed drying, while very humid air can slow it. Drying does not require water to boil. Spreading out a cloth increases its exposed surface area.

Problem data. The table of observations is: the cloth A: is stretched, is in an air current, starts with the same water; the cloth B: is stretched, starts with the same water; the towel C: is in an air current; the paper D: is stretched; the sponge E: is in an air current, starts with the same water. A peer states: “any case with the property “starts with the same water” also has the property “is stretched”.”

Question. Is the universal statement true in the given data? If not, provide a counterexample and explain exactly which part of the statement it refutes.
