# 39 — Choice under multiple conditions

Problem world. In our model, a sensory receptor detects a stimulus, sends a nerve signal, the control center interprets the information, and muscles can produce a response. If the signal does not reach the control center, a response based on that signal cannot be chosen correctly. A fast response is not automatically a correct response; the information must first be detected and transmitted.

Case data. Option A: the stimulus is detected: YES; the signal is transmitted: YES; the information is interpreted: YES; the muscle can respond: NO. Option B: the stimulus is detected: YES; the signal is transmitted: NO; the information is interpreted: YES; the muscle can respond: YES. Option C: the stimulus is detected: NO; the signal is transmitted: YES; the information is interpreted: YES; the muscle can respond: YES. Option D: the stimulus is detected: YES; the signal is transmitted: YES; the information is interpreted: YES; the muscle can respond: YES.

Question. Choose the option that simultaneously meets all conditions: the information is interpreted, the muscle can respond, the signal is transmitted, the stimulus is detected. If even one mandatory condition is NO, the option must be eliminated. Which one remains, and why can the “count the most YES values” method give a wrong answer?
