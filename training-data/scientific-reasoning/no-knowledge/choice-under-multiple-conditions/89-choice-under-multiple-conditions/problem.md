# 89 — Choice under multiple conditions

Problem world. In a simple-circuit model, a battery, wires, and a bulb must form a closed path for the bulb to light. A break anywhere along the path opens the circuit. Some materials conduct and can bridge a gap; others are insulators. We do not use details about voltage or current; every conclusion follows only from the idea of a continuous path.

Case data. Option A: the battery is connected: YES; the wires form a path continuous: YES; the bulb is connected: YES; the switch is closed: NO. Option B: the battery is connected: YES; the wires form a path continuous: NO; the bulb is connected: YES; the switch is closed: YES. Option C: the battery is connected: NO; the wires form a path continuous: YES; the bulb is connected: YES; the switch is closed: YES. Option D: the battery is connected: YES; the wires form a path continuous: YES; the bulb is connected: YES; the switch is closed: YES.

Question. Choose the option that simultaneously meets all conditions: the battery is connected, the bulb is connected, the wires form a path continuous, the switch is closed. If even one mandatory condition is NO, the option must be eliminated. Which one remains, and why can the “count the most YES values” method give a wrong answer?
