# 928 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Two gears that touch directly rotate in opposite directions. In the model, if a small gear drives a larger gear, the larger gear makes fewer rotations in the same time; an odd or even number of contacts can change the final direction. The teeth prevent slipping in the ideal model. An intermediate gear can change direction without being the useful output.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “has 10 teeth” and “is in direct contact with A” occur together with an increase in the quantity “output rotations”. Test A changes only “has 10 teeth” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “is in direct contact with A” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
