# 898 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Animals can respond to seasons through migration, hibernation, storing food, or changing their coat. In the model, a strategy succeeds if the animal secures energy, protection, and access to resources under the season’s conditions. Migration means seasonal movement between regions. Hibernation greatly reduces activity during certain periods.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “migrates” and “stores food” occur together with an increase in the quantity “energy reserve”. Test A changes only “migrates” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “stores food” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
