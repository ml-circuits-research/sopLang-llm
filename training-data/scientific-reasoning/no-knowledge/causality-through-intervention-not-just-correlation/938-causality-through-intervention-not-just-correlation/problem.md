# 938 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. For equal volumes, the material with smaller mass has lower density. In the model, an object floats in water if its average density is lower than the density of water; a hollow shape can contain air and reduce average density. A compact piece of metal can be denser than water. A metal ship can float because its total volume contains a great deal of air.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “has an air cavity” and “has a large volume” occur together with an increase in the quantity “relative density”. Test A changes only “has an air cavity” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “has a large volume” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
