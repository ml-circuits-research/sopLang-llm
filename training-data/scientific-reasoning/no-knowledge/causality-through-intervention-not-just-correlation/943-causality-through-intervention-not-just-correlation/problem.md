# 943 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Air occupies space and can be compressed. In a model of a closed syringe, pushing the plunger decreases the volume of the air and increases pressure; if the air has a path out, pressure does not increase in the same way. Air is matter even though we cannot see it. A balloon can stretch when the pressure inside increases.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “keeps air trapped” and “can change its volume” occur together with an increase in the quantity “air volume”. Test A changes only “keeps air trapped” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “can change its volume” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
