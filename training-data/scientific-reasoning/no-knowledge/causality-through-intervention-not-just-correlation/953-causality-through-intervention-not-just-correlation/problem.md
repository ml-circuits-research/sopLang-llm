# 953 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. A mirror does not produce light; it changes the direction of light that reaches its surface. In the grid-ray model, the outgoing angle is symmetric with the incoming angle relative to the normal to the mirror. Light travels in straight lines in the uniform medium of the model. A matte surface scatters light in many directions.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “reflects toward right” and “is diagonally oriented” occur together with an increase in the quantity “route length”. Test A changes only “reflects toward right” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “is diagonally oriented” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
