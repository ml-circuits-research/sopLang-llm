# 973 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. In the model, the Moon does not produce its own visible light; it reflects sunlight. Half of the Moon is illuminated by the Sun, and the phase seen from Earth depends on the relative positions of the Sun, Earth, and Moon. Moon phases are not caused by Earth’s shadow; Earth’s shadow matters during a lunar eclipse. The Moon travels in orbit around Earth.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “shows the illuminated part” and “is opposite the Sun” occur together with an increase in the quantity “visible illuminated fraction”. Test A changes only “shows the illuminated part” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “is opposite the Sun” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
