# 908 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Incisors cut, canines can tear, and molars crush and grind. Enamel protects the outside of the tooth. In the model, preparing a piece of food well requires cutting or tearing and then grinding before swallowing. Teeth have different shapes for different mechanical roles. Chewing increases the surface area of pieces of food.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “can cut” and “can crush” occur together with an increase in the quantity “average piece size”. Test A changes only “can cut” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “can crush” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
