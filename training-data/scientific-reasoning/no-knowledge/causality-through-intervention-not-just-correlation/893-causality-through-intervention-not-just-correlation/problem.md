# 893 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Soil is a mixture that may contain mineral particles, humus, water, and air. In the model, a good layer for roots must retain some water while also leaving air spaces; too much water without air, or drainage that is too fast, can create problems. Sand generally has larger particles than clay. Humus comes from transformed organic remains.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “drains quickly” and “contains humus” occur together with an increase in the quantity “time of infiltration”. Test A changes only “drains quickly” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “contains humus” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
