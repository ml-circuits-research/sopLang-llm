# 948 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Evaporation occurs at the surface of a liquid. In the model, a larger surface area, moving air, and a higher temperature can speed drying, while very humid air can slow it. Drying does not require water to boil. Spreading out a cloth increases its exposed surface area.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “is stretched” and “is in an air current” occur together with an increase in the quantity “water remaining”. Test A changes only “is stretched” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “is in an air current” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
