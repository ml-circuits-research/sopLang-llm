# 918 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Microorganisms can multiply on food when they find suitable conditions. In the model, cooling slows multiplication, drying reduces available water, and a clean container reduces initial contamination; none of these measures means that a food becomes sterile. Heat and moisture can speed some biological processes. “Cold” means slowing in the model, not absolute stopping.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “is cooled” and “is dry” occur together with an increase in the quantity “index of spoilage”. Test A changes only “is cooled” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “is dry” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
