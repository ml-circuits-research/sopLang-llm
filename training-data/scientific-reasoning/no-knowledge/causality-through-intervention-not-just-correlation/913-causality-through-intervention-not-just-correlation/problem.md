# 913 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. During exercise, muscles need more oxygen and energy, and pulse and breathing may increase. after exercise stops, the values tend to return gradually toward the resting level. In the model, recovery is tracked by measurements taken at the same time intervals. Sweating can contribute to water loss. A single measurement does not describe the whole recovery process.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “has complete measurements” and “made the same effort” occur together with an increase in the quantity “pulse in the model”. Test A changes only “has complete measurements” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “made the same effort” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
