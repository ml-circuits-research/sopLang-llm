# 963 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. In the circuit model, the metals used here are conductors, while plastic and rubber are insulators. Current appears only if there is a continuous conducting path between the source terminals; insulation is used to prevent contact with conducting parts. A conductor allows current to pass in the model. An insulator breaks the electrical path.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “is conductor” and “is insulated on the outside” occur together with an increase in the quantity “number of correct contacts”. Test A changes only “is conductor” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “is insulated on the outside” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
