# 888 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. Fungi and bacteria can decompose organic remains. In the model, active decomposition needs decomposers, enough water, and a suitable temperature; the matter in the remains is transformed and some substances return to the soil. Dead leaves do not disappear instantly; decomposition has stages. Strong drying can slow the activity of many decomposers.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “is moist” and “is broken into smaller pieces” occur together with an increase in the quantity “remaining mass”. Test A changes only “is moist” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase disappears. Test B changes only “is broken into smaller pieces” from present to absent; the increase remains.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
