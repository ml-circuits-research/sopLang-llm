# 983 — Causality through intervention, not just correlation

Given knowledge. In the hiking model, the map shows distances and elevation changes, weather can change speed, and the team has a limited reserve of water and energy. Layered clothing reduces heat loss under the stated cold conditions. Map scale allows map distance to be converted into real distance. Climbing uses more energy in the model than walking on level ground.

Problem data. In a subexperiment, the factors “is short” and “has a small elevation gain” occur together with an increase in the quantity “travel time”. Test A changes only “is short” from present to absent, with all other conditions held constant; the increase remains. Test B changes only “has a small elevation gain” from present to absent; the increase disappears.

Question. Which factor is supported as causal in this submodel? Which factor remains merely associated? Explain why the intervention is more informative than the initial observation.
