# Explanation 891 — Structural analogy between systems

## Explanation

1. The chain of the natural system is fixed by the order of its steps: 1. the same amount of water is poured, 2. water enters the pores, 3. a part is retained, 4. part drains away.
2. The artificial system names the same four positions in the same order: Source → Preparation Station → Channel → Collector.
3. Pairing the steps with the roles in that order preserves the causal links, because each step prepares or allows the next one, exactly as each role hands the object to the next.
4. The correspondence survives a change of material: only the number of positions and the order of the relationships are compared, not the appearance of the parts.

Reference solution as printed in the source (form 36, 4 steps):

1. The first element, “the same amount of water is poured”, is the beginning of the chain, therefore corresponds to the Source.
2. “water enters the pores” is the second role that prepares the next transition, therefore corresponds to the Preparation Station.
3. “a part is retained” is the link through which the effect reaches the end, therefore we map it to the Channel; “part drains away” corresponds to the Collector.
4. The analogy preserves the order of relationships and the causal/functional roles, not the material or shape of the components.

## Result

**Answer.** The same amount of water is poured↔ Source; water enters the pores↔ Preparation Station; a part is retained↔ Channel; part drains away↔ Collector. It preserves the structure of the relationships.
