# 98 — If-then logical chain

Problem world. In the water-cycle model, liquid water can evaporate, water vapor can cool and condense into droplets, and sufficiently large droplets can fall as precipitation. Fallen water can collect in rivers, lakes, or soil and later re-enter the cycle. Clouds are not explained as “water smoke,” but as very small droplets formed after cooling and condensation.

Case data. The local rules form a single chain: In the model, if “water evaporates” has been completed, then “the vapor rises” can begin. In the model, if “the vapor rises” has been completed, then “the vapor cools and condenses” can begin. In the model, if “the vapor cools and condenses” has been completed, then “the droplets can fall and be collected” can begin. We know that “water evaporates” has been completed.

Question. What conclusions can we deduce successively? Show why each conclusion becomes a premise for the next rule.
