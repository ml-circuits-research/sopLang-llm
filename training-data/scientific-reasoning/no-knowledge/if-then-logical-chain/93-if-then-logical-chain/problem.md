# 93 — If-then logical chain

Problem world. In the model, air occupies space, water flows through open spaces, and soils differ in how quickly they allow water to pass. Soil with different particles and spaces may retain more or less water; this property is measured in the test rather than guessed. To compare two soils, we pour the same amount of water and wait the same amount of time.

Case data. The local rules form a single chain: In the model, if “we pour water onto the soil” has been completed, then “water enters the spaces between particles” can begin. In the model, if “water enters the spaces between particles” has been completed, then “a part is retained” can begin. In the model, if “a part is retained” has been completed, then “the remainder drains away” can begin. We know that “we pour water onto the soil” has been completed.

Question. What conclusions can we deduce successively? Show why each conclusion becomes a premise for the next rule.
