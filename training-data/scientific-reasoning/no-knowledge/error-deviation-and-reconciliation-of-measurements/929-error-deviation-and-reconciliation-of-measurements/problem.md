# 929 — Error, deviation, and reconciliation of measurements

Given knowledge. Two gears that touch directly rotate in opposite directions. In the model, if a small gear drives a larger gear, the larger gear makes fewer rotations in the same time; an odd or even number of contacts can change the final direction. The teeth prevent slipping in the ideal model. An intermediate gear can change direction without being the useful output.

Problem data. Repeated measurements for “output rotations” (rotations/min): 20, 21, 20, 19, 21, 30. The sheet states that, in a stable system, the repeated measurements tend to form a group with small differences; a series that changes steadily can indicate drift rather than a single bad point.

Question. Analyze the shape of the series: is there an isolated suspicious point, a gradual drift or none? What should you check before changing the data? Give a robust estimate of the center of the series when appropriate.
