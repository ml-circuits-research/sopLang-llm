# 693 — Intervals, bounds, and possibility

Given knowledge. Air occupies space and can be compressed. In a model of a closed syringe, pushing the plunger decreases the volume of the air and increases pressure; if the air has a path out, pressure does not increase in the same way. Air is matter even though we cannot see it. A balloon can stretch when the pressure inside increases.

Problem data. The accepted zone for “air volume” is the closed interval [7,11] mL; each result is known only as interval: A=[7,8]; B=[10,12]; C=[12,14]; D=[5,6].

Question. For each sample, say whether it is certainly accepted, certainly rejected, or still uncertain. Then identify which sample would benefit most from a more precise measurement.
