# 154 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In the simplified model used here, food is broken into smaller pieces in the mouth, reaches the stomach, and then the small intestine, where much of the nutrients pass into the blood. In the large intestine, some water is recovered from the remaining material. The order of the organs matters: a substance cannot reach a later part of the intestine before passing through earlier parts of the route.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: problem is before the intestine predicts [low absorption, food does not reach the intestine, the stomach receives food], while H2: the problem is intestinal absorption predicts [low absorption, food reaches the intestine, the preceding digestive path is normal]. The shared observation “low absorption” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
