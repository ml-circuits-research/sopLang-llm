# 244 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In the teaching model, foods can provide energy and substances needed to build and operate the body. A balanced meal is not decided by a single food but by the combination and amount. For this problem, each option has explicitly stated properties; no real food values need to be memorized. We track three requirements at the same time: enough energy, a source of protein, and a source of fiber.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: the lack of satiety comes from a portion with too little energy predicts [increasing the portion helps, energy below target, protein may be present], while H2: the lack comes from unsuitable composition predicts [energy at target, the required component is missing, changing the composition helps]. The shared observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
