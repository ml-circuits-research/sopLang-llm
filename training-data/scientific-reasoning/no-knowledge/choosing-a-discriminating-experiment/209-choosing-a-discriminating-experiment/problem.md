# 209 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In the model, a magnet can attract certain magnetic materials without direct contact. Two magnet ends can attract or repel depending on orientation. We do not assume that all metals are magnetic; the result of the test is stated explicitly for every object. A thin nonmagnetic barrier does not necessarily stop the magnet’s effect in our model.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: the object is not magnetic predicts [near of magnet, the magnet attracts another object, does not moves], while H2: the magnet is too far predicts [does not move far, object known magnetic, moves then when we bring closer the magnet]. The shared observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
