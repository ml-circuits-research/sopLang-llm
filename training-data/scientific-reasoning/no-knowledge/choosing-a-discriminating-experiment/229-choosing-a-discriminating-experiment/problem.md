# 229 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In the model, Earth is approximated as a sphere rotating around its axis. The side facing the Sun is illuminated, while the opposite side is in night. As Earth rotates, a place can move from the illuminated region to the dark region and back again. These problems do not require astronomical distances or real durations; we use only the stated geometric model.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: alternation comes from rotation of the sphere predicts [the point marked enters and goes out from light, rotation changes the state, the source remains fixed], while H2: alternation comes from turning off the source predicts [rotation is not necessary, the source turns off, all the points darkens simultaneously]. The shared observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
