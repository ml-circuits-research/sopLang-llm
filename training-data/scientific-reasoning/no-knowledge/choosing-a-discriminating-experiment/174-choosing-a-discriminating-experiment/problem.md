# 174 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. Choosing a material depends on the properties required by the object, such as waterproofness, flexibility, transparency, or thermal insulation. No material is “the best” in every situation; it is suitable if it meets the requirements of its use at the same time. In this problem, the properties are stated explicitly and do not need to be guessed from everyday experience.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: the loss of heat is through material predicts [the container remains closed, temperature decreases quickly, a layer insulator helps], while H2: the loss is through opening predicts [temperature decreases quickly, the wall type matters little, closing helps]. The shared observation “temperature decreases quickly” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
