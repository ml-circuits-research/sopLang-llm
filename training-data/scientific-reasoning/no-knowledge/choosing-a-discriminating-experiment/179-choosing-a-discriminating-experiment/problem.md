# 179 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. Our model mixture contains iron filings, pebbles, sand, and salt dissolved in water. A magnet attracts the iron filings; a sieve separates larger particles from smaller ones; filtration can retain sand while water passes through; evaporating the water can leave the salt behind. A method must be chosen according to the property that differs between the components.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: the the liquid is cloudy because of the sand predicts [the filter clarifies it, the magnet does not change the turbidity, sand on filter], while H2: the the liquid is cloudy because of the fine iron particles predicts [the filter can retain them as well, the magnet reduces the particles, particles magnetic]. The shared observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
