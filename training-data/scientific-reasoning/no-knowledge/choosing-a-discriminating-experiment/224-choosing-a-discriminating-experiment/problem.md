# 224 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In the water-cycle model, liquid water can evaporate, water vapor can cool and condense into droplets, and sufficiently large droplets can fall as precipitation. Fallen water can collect in rivers, lakes, or soil and later re-enter the cycle. Clouds are not explained as “water smoke,” but as very small droplets formed after cooling and condensation.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: there is too little water vapor predicts [low evaporation, little water vapor, heating increases the droplets], while H2: the water vapor does not cool predicts [much water vapor, few droplets, cooling increases condensation]. The shared observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
