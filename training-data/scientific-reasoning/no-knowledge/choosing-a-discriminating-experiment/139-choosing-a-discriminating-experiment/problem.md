# 139 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In the small meadow described here, grass is eaten by rabbits, and rabbits can be eaten by foxes. If the food available to one group decreases greatly, that group has fewer resources; the effect can then continue along the chain. We will not assume that animal numbers change instantly: we track only the likely direction of the effect after some time.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: rabbits decrease from lack of food predicts [little grass, rabbits decrease, the tracks of fox must not increase], while H2: rabbits decrease through predation predicts [grass sufficient, rabbits decrease, the tracks of fox increase]. The shared observation “rabbits decrease” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
