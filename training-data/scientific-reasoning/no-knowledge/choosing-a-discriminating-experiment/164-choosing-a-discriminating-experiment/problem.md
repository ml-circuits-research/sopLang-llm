# 164 — Choosing a discriminating experiment

Problem world. In our model, a sensory receptor detects a stimulus, sends a nerve signal, the control center interprets the information, and muscles can produce a response. If the signal does not reach the control center, a response based on that signal cannot be chosen correctly. A fast response is not automatically a correct response; the information must first be detected and transmitted.

Case data. We have two explanations: H1: the receiver does not detect predicts [without signal initial, reaction absent, stimulus stronger helps], while H2: the path of signal is blocked predicts [the center does not receive, signal initial there is, stimulus stronger does not help]. The shared observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located” does not distinguish them.

Question. Choose a test that would produce a different observation for the two hypotheses. What should we check, and what result would support each hypothesis?
