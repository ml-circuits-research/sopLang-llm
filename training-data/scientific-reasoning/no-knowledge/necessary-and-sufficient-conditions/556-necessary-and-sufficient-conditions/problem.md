# 556 — Necessary and sufficient conditions

Given knowledge. An object remains stable in the model if the projection of its center of mass stays above its base of support. A wider base or moving mass inward can increase stability. In the model, the center of mass is the point at which we can treat mass as concentrated for balance analysis. Raising mass higher can make the system easier to tip.

Problem data. The target result is “the system remains stable”. In this model, it occurs exactly when three conditions are met simultaneously: A=the center of mass is above the base; B=the base of support does not slip; C=the load is secured. Case P has only C=YES and the other two conditions NO; Case Q has C=NO and the other two YES; Case R has A=B=C=YES.

Question. A peer state: “if C is true, the result is guaranteed”. Another peer state: “without C, the result is impossible”. Which statement is correct and why?
