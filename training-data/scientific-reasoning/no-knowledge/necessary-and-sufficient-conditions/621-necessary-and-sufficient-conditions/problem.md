# 621 — Necessary and sufficient conditions

Given knowledge. In the kitchen model, heating transfers energy, dissolving forms a homogeneous mixture only for certain substances and quantities, and clean utensils reduce contamination. Experimental recipes can be compared only when quantities and times are measured in the same way. A dissolved substance has not disappeared; it is distributed through the solvent. Stirring can speed dissolving without changing the maximum amount allowed by the model.

Problem data. The target result is “the culinary experiment is comparable and controlled”. In this model, it occurs exactly when three conditions are met simultaneously: A=the quantities are measured in the same way; B=temperature is controlled; C=the utensils are clean. Case P has only A=YES and the other two conditions NO; Case Q has A=NO and the other two YES; Case R has A=B=C=YES.

Question. Analyze the condition A=“the quantities are measured in the same way”. Is it necessary for the result? Is it sufficient on its own? What do cases P, Q, and R show?
