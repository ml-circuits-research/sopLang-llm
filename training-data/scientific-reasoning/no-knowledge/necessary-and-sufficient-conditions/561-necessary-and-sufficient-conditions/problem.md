# 561 — Necessary and sufficient conditions

Given knowledge. For equal volumes, the material with smaller mass has lower density. In the model, an object floats in water if its average density is lower than the density of water; a hollow shape can contain air and reduce average density. A compact piece of metal can be denser than water. A metal ship can float because its total volume contains a great deal of air.

Problem data. The target result is “the object floats”. In this model, it occurs exactly when three conditions are met simultaneously: A=average density is below that of water; B=the object does not take water into the air cavity; C=the volume is maintained. Case P has only A=YES and the other two conditions NO; Case Q has A=NO and the other two YES; Case R has A=B=C=YES.

Question. Analyze the condition A=“average density is below that of water”. Is it necessary for the result? Is it sufficient on its own? What do cases P, Q, and R show?
