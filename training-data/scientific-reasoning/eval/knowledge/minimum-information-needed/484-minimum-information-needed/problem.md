# 484 — Minimum information needed

Problem world. In our model, rock fragments can be broken off by weathering, transported by water or wind, and then deposited when the transporting agent loses energy. Repeated deposits can form layers. Traces or remains of organisms can become trapped in sediments and, under certain conditions, become fossils. A fossil does not form instantly; the problem uses only the logical order of the stages, not their real duration.

Case data. after the observation “the observed problem exists, but its cause has not yet been located”, both hypotheses remain possible: H1: the lack of the layer comes from transport that is too strong and H2: the lack of the layer comes from a lack of fragments. We can make one additional observation.

Question. What is the minimum information that would separate the hypotheses? Explain why a shared observation does not help, while a discriminating one does.
