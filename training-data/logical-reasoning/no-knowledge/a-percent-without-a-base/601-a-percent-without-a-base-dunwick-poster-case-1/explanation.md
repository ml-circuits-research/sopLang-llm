# Explanation 601 — A percent without a base — Dunwick poster — case 1

## Explanation

1. The poster in Dunwick announces a cut of 10%, but a percent is a sentence with a hole: it is always a percent of something, and no original price, date, or basket is listed.
2. Sam treats the poster as a finished quantity, which reads a number the page never printed.
3. Tess keeps the hole open and asks for the base, and the “down” also needs the date it is measured from.
4. Owen fills the hole with a base of 100 to be helpful, but a helpful invention is still an invention: it writes a second poster instead of reading this one.

Reference solution as printed in the source (section 61, 5 steps):

1. A percent is of something.
2. That something is missing.
3. Helpful invention is still invention.
4. “Down” also needs “from when.”
5. Write “unknown base,” not a fake number.

## Result

**Answer.** Only a bare percent. Inventing 100 writes a second poster.
