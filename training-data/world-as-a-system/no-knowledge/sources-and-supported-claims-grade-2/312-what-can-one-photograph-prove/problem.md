# 312 — What can one photograph prove?

Knowledge context. Historical sources are evidence, not automatic answers. The strength of a conclusion depends on what the source actually records.

Given facts. A historian has one source: a photograph. The source directly shows that a scene was photographed. A student proposes two claims: C1: a scene was photographed; C2: the whole population agreed with the ruler.

Rules. A source supports only claims that can reasonably be inferred from what it contains. Absence of evidence for a broad claim is not proof of that claim.

Task. Which claim is directly supported, and which is not justified by this source alone?
