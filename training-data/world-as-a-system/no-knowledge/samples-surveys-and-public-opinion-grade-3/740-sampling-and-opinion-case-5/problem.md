# 740 — Sampling and opinion: case 5

Knowledge context. Surveys use samples to learn about larger populations. The selection process matters as much as the number of answers.

Given facts. An online poll is open to anyone who chooses to click it. Cross-domain check: on a field sketch, 1 cm represents 2 km and two checkpoints are 7 cm apart.

Rules. A sample is more representative when selection does not systematically favor people connected to the answer. Random or broad systematic sampling can reduce selection bias; voluntary or topic-specific locations may create bias. For the cross-domain check, real distance = sketch distance × kilometres per centimetre.

Task. Is the sampling method likely to be biased for the stated question? Explain the direction of the possible bias. Cross-domain check: what real distance separates the checkpoints?
