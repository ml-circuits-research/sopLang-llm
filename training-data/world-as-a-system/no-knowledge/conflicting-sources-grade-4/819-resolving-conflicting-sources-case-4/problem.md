# 819 — Resolving conflicting sources: case 4

Knowledge context. Historical accounts can disagree. Reasoning requires evaluating how a source knows what it claims, not simply counting statements.

Given facts. Source A, an eyewitness diary, says the bridge opened on Monday. Source B, a later textbook, says Tuesday. Source C, an independent dated toll record, begins charging on Monday. Cross-domain check: researchers collected 15 reports, but 4 are exact duplicate copies that add no new independent information. Mixed-domain verification: a regional archive has 83 map sheets and receives 10 additional sheets during the exercise.

Rules. When sources conflict, compare proximity to the event, independence, and whether a source has a reason or mechanism to record the specific fact. Agreement between independent relevant sources increases support but does not create absolute certainty. For the cross-domain check, independent reports = total reports − duplicate copies. For this verification, the final number of sheets equals starting sheets plus received sheets.

Task. Which account is better supported by the evidence given, and why? Cross-domain check: how many non-duplicate reports remain? Mixed-domain verification: how many sheets are available after the delivery?
