# 943 — Provenance and chain of custody: case 3

Knowledge context. Provenance records where an object came from and how it moved between custodians.

Given facts. An artifact record states: found by Excavator on day 1; transferred to Museum A on day 2; sent from Museum A to Research Lab on day 5; returned by Research Lab to Museum B on day 8. Every transfer must name both sender and receiver. Cross-domain check: researchers collected 9 reports, but 4 are exact duplicate copies that add no new independent information. Mixed-domain verification: a regional archive has 96 map sheets and receives 4 additional sheets during the exercise.

Rules. A continuous chain of custody requires each handoff to connect the previous holder to the next. A missing sender or receiver creates a gap that limits certainty about continuous custody. For the cross-domain check, independent reports = total reports − duplicate copies. For this verification, the final number of sheets equals starting sheets plus received sheets.

Task. Is the stated chain of custody complete under the rule? If not, identify the gap. Cross-domain check: how many non-duplicate reports remain? Mixed-domain verification: how many sheets are available after the delivery?
