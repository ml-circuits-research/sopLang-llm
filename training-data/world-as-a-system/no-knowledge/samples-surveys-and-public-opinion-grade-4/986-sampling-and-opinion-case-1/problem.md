# 986 — Sampling and opinion: case 1

Knowledge context. Surveys use samples to learn about larger populations. The selection process matters as much as the number of answers.

Given facts. A town wants to estimate how many residents use buses. It surveys only people waiting at the bus station. Cross-domain check: a local committee has 13 members, requires at least 7 present for quorum, and 11 are present.

Rules. A sample is more representative when selection does not systematically favor people connected to the answer. Random or broad systematic sampling can reduce selection bias; voluntary or topic-specific locations may create bias. For the cross-domain check, quorum exists when present members ≥ the stated threshold.

Task. Is the sampling method likely to be biased for the stated question? Explain the direction of the possible bias. Cross-domain check: is quorum met?
