# 987 — Sampling and opinion: case 2

Knowledge context. Surveys use samples to learn about larger populations. The selection process matters as much as the number of answers.

Given facts. A school wants opinions on lunch. It randomly selects 10 students from each grade. Cross-domain check: researchers collected 13 reports, but 4 are exact duplicate copies that add no new independent information. Mixed-domain verification: a regional archive has 100 map sheets and receives 8 additional sheets during the exercise.

Rules. A sample is more representative when selection does not systematically favor people connected to the answer. Random or broad systematic sampling can reduce selection bias; voluntary or topic-specific locations may create bias. For the cross-domain check, independent reports = total reports − duplicate copies. For this verification, the final number of sheets equals starting sheets plus received sheets.

Task. Is the sampling method likely to be biased for the stated question? Explain the direction of the possible bias. Cross-domain check: how many non-duplicate reports remain? Mixed-domain verification: how many sheets are available after the delivery?
