# 481 — Procedure, evidence, and a right to respond: case 1

Knowledge context. Fair procedures often combine evidentiary standards with an opportunity for the affected person to answer the case.

Given facts. A school rule says a student may receive a serious penalty only if: (1) at least two independent pieces of evidence support the allegation, and (2) the student is given a chance to respond before the final decision. In a case, one camera record and one independent witness support the allegation, but the student was not allowed to respond. Cross-domain check: a survey team starts work at 8:00 and works for 2 hour(s) without a break. Mixed-domain verification: a regional archive has 50 map sheets and receives 2 additional sheets during the exercise.

Rules. Independence concerns distinct information sources, not merely the number of people repeating one source. Every required procedural condition must be satisfied. For the cross-domain check, finishing time = starting time + duration. For this verification, the final number of sheets equals starting sheets plus received sheets.

Task. May the serious penalty be imposed under the stated procedure? Identify the condition that succeeds or fails. Cross-domain check: at what time does the team finish? Mixed-domain verification: how many sheets are available after the delivery?
