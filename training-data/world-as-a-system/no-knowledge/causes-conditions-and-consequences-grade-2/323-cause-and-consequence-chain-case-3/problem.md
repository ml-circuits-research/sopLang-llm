# 323 — Cause-and-consequence chain: case 3

Knowledge context. Many explanations can be represented as condition-and-consequence rules. The logical form matters: necessary, sufficient, AND, and OR are not interchangeable.

Given facts. Facts: Rain occurred; The river was already high; The warning siren worked; The gate was closed; Flooding occurred. Rules: If rain occurs AND the river is already high, flooding occurs. If flooding occurs, the riverside road closes. If the siren works, residents receive a warning. Cross-domain check: researchers collected 9 reports, but 4 are exact duplicate copies that add no new independent information.

Rules. Use the rules exactly as written. AND requires all listed conditions; OR requires at least one. A one-way implication cannot automatically be reversed. For the cross-domain check, independent reports = total reports − duplicate copies.

Task. Which consequences can be deduced by applying the rules exactly? Do not assume the converse of any rule. Cross-domain check: how many non-duplicate reports remain?
