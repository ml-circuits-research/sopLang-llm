# 322 — Cause-and-consequence chain: case 2

Knowledge context. Many explanations can be represented as condition-and-consequence rules. The logical form matters: necessary, sufficient, AND, and OR are not interchangeable.

Given facts. Facts: Rain occurred; The river was not high; The warning siren worked; The gate was closed. Rules: If rain occurs AND the river is already high, flooding occurs. If flooding occurs, the riverside road closes. If the siren works, residents receive a warning. Cross-domain check: a local committee has 15 members, requires at least 8 present for quorum, and 14 are present.

Rules. Use the rules exactly as written. AND requires all listed conditions; OR requires at least one. A one-way implication cannot automatically be reversed. For the cross-domain check, quorum exists when present members ≥ the stated threshold.

Task. Which consequences can be deduced by applying the rules exactly? Do not assume the converse of any rule. Cross-domain check: is quorum met?
