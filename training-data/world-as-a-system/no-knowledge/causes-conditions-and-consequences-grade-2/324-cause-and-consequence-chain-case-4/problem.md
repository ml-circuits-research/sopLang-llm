# 324 — Cause-and-consequence chain: case 4

Knowledge context. Many explanations can be represented as condition-and-consequence rules. The logical form matters: necessary, sufficient, AND, and OR are not interchangeable.

Given facts. Facts: Rain occurred; The river was already high; The warning siren worked; The gate was closed. Rules: If rain occurs OR the river is already high, the field becomes muddy. If flooding occurs, the riverside road closes. If the siren works, residents receive a warning. Cross-domain check: on a field sketch, 1 cm represents 6 km and two checkpoints are 4 cm apart.

Rules. Use the rules exactly as written. AND requires all listed conditions; OR requires at least one. A one-way implication cannot automatically be reversed. For the cross-domain check, real distance = sketch distance × kilometres per centimetre.

Task. Which consequences can be deduced by applying the rules exactly? Do not assume the converse of any rule. Cross-domain check: what real distance separates the checkpoints?
