# 696 — Perspective, interest, and source quality: case 1

Knowledge context. Sources have perspectives and interests. Credibility is not a simple label; it depends on the claim being evaluated and how the source could know it.

Given facts. Three sources describe a proposed market tax. Source A is a merchant who would pay the tax and argues it is harmful. Source B is a council report that estimates revenue and lists its calculation. Source C is a traveler repeating a rumor heard in another town. Cross-domain check: on a field sketch, 1 cm represents 3 km and two checkpoints are 5 cm apart.

Rules. Perspective and interest do not automatically make a source false. Evaluate relevance, access to information, explicit evidence, and independence. A source with an interest may still provide useful facts, but its claims should be checked. For the cross-domain check, real distance = sketch distance × kilometres per centimetre.

Task. Which source is best positioned to estimate the amount of revenue, and which source should be treated most cautiously for that numerical claim? Cross-domain check: what real distance separates the checkpoints?
