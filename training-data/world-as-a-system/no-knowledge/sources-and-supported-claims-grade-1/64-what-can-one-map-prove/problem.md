# 64 — What can one map prove?

Knowledge context. Historical sources are evidence, not automatic answers. The strength of a conclusion depends on what the source actually records.

Given facts. A historian has one source: a map. The source directly shows that a road was mapped. A student proposes two claims: C1: a road was mapped; C2: the whole population agreed with the ruler. Cross-domain check: on a field sketch, 1 cm represents 6 km and two checkpoints are 3 cm apart.

Rules. A source supports only claims that can reasonably be inferred from what it contains. Absence of evidence for a broad claim is not proof of that claim. For the cross-domain check, real distance = sketch distance × kilometres per centimetre.

Task. Which claim is directly supported, and which is not justified by this source alone? Cross-domain check: what real distance separates the checkpoints?
