# 831 — Change and continuity in a settlement: case 1

Knowledge context. History studies both change and continuity. Comparing two snapshots is a systematic way to separate what persisted from what appeared or disappeared.

Given facts. Period 1 features: footpath, market, well, wooden bridge. Period 2 features: market, road, stone bridge, well. Cross-domain check: researchers collected 7 reports, but 4 are exact duplicate copies that add no new independent information. Mixed-domain verification: a regional archive has 85 map sheets and receives 2 additional sheets during the exercise.

Rules. A continuity is a feature present in both periods. A disappearance is present only in Period 1. An appearance is present only in Period 2. A replacement can be described when two differently named features serve the same stated role. For the cross-domain check, independent reports = total reports − duplicate copies. For this verification, the final number of sheets equals starting sheets plus received sheets.

Task. Identify the continuities, disappearances, and appearances. Which conclusions are direct set comparisons rather than interpretations? Cross-domain check: how many non-duplicate reports remain? Mixed-domain verification: how many sheets are available after the delivery?
