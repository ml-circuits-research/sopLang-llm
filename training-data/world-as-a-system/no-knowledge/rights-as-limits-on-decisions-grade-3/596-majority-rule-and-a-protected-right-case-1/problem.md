# 596 — Majority rule and a protected right: case 1

Knowledge context. Democratic procedures use voting, but constitutional systems can place some rights beyond ordinary majority power.

Given facts. A council of 20 members votes 15 to 5 for a proposal that would forbid one named group from the right to speak at a peaceful meeting. The classroom constitution states: (1) ordinary proposals pass by simple majority; (2) an ordinary majority may not remove the listed equal rights from a group.

Rules. A decision must satisfy both the voting procedure and the higher rights constraint. Passing a vote is not sufficient if the content violates a protected right.

Task. Did the proposal receive a majority? May it take effect under the stated constitution?
