# 70 — Resolving conflicting sources: case 5

Knowledge context. Historical accounts can disagree. Reasoning requires evaluating how a source knows what it claims, not simply counting statements.

Given facts. Source A, written on the day of an event, says 40 carts arrived. Source B, copied 80 years later from an unknown account, says 90 carts arrived. Source C is an independent warehouse record from the same week and lists 42 carts. Cross-domain check: a local committee has 15 members, requires at least 8 present for quorum, and 14 are present.

Rules. When sources conflict, compare proximity to the event, independence, and whether a source has a reason or mechanism to record the specific fact. Agreement between independent relevant sources increases support but does not create absolute certainty. For the cross-domain check, quorum exists when present members ≥ the stated threshold.

Task. Which account is better supported by the evidence given, and why? Cross-domain check: is quorum met?
