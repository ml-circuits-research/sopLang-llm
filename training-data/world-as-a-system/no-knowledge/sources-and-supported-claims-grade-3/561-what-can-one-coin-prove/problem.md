# 561 — What can one coin prove?

Knowledge context. Historical sources are evidence, not automatic answers. The strength of a conclusion depends on what the source actually records.

Given facts. A historian has one source: a coin. The source directly shows that people used metal objects. A student proposes two claims: C1: people used metal objects; C2: the whole population agreed with the ruler. Cross-domain check: a survey team starts work at 8:00 and works for 2 hour(s) without a break. Mixed-domain verification: a regional archive has 58 map sheets and receives 2 additional sheets during the exercise.

Rules. A source supports only claims that can reasonably be inferred from what it contains. Absence of evidence for a broad claim is not proof of that claim. For the cross-domain check, finishing time = starting time + duration. For this verification, the final number of sheets equals starting sheets plus received sheets.

Task. Which claim is directly supported, and which is not justified by this source alone? Cross-domain check: at what time does the team finish? Mixed-domain verification: how many sheets are available after the delivery?
