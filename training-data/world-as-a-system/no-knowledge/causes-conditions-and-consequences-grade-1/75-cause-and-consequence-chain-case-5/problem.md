# 75 — Cause-and-consequence chain: case 5

Knowledge context. Many explanations can be represented as condition-and-consequence rules. The logical form matters: necessary, sufficient, AND, and OR are not interchangeable.

Given facts. Facts: Rain occurred; The river was already high. Rules: If rain AND high river, flooding occurs. If flooding, road closes. If road closes, the bus reroutes.

Rules. Use the rules exactly as written. AND requires all listed conditions; OR requires at least one. A one-way implication cannot automatically be reversed.

Task. Which consequences can be deduced by applying the rules exactly? Do not assume the converse of any rule.
