### B1. REJECT-level for 04 as written: the stop rule did not halt training; it was overridden within about two hours by the goal change the paper criticises
- 04 Abstract: "a stop rule that halted training until the benchmark was shown to be sound". 04 Section 4.1: "The stop rule worked as intended ... it made that question answerable before more compute was spent ... [it] could not be reinterpreted afterwards." Section 6: "These were the moments at which the programme was most robust."
- The rule, as revised at 19:02 after it fired (52249feb7, `proposal.md`): "2. Re-run this agent-eval on the SAME 20 items. The pass criterion: a competent writer reaches 20 of 20. Until then, no training, no further arms. 3. Only after the benchmark passes the writer test do we reconsider training the model." The original (18:55) also said "We do not launch another training arm on a restructured dataset".
- What happened: 20:37 plan (143605893) schedules "train qwen2.5-coder-0.5B on the fixed data ... If the 0.5B disappoints, the 1.7B is retrained tomorrow"; 21:01 dv13 dataset change (df3602594: "units-and-rates taught, integer-probe contamination eliminated"); exp-026-0.5b-qwen2.5coder-dv13 was trained (training manifest created 2026-09-26T19:03Z, i.e. 21:03 local, two minutes after the dv13 commit) and evaluated that night (evaluation started 20:14Z; verdict a3572074c 22:23 local, "361/705"); exp-027-1.7b-qwen3-dv13 trained overnight (verdict c6d508b95 at 02:35, "421/705"). The "value-based scorer" was an agent rescoring (see B3), not the re-test the rule required.
- So the programme's own record shows the pre-declared stop rule lasting under two hours before a goal change reopened training. This is the paper's strongest evidence *for* its thesis (goal pressure overrides pre-declared governance), and it is currently presented as a success story. A reviewer who reads the git log will conclude the paper selectively reports; in a paper about selective reading this is fatal.
- Fix: rewrite 4.1 as "a stop rule that worked once and was then overridden": the rule caught the instrument problem (true), then the 20:37 pivot launched two training runs on a changed dataset without the re-test the rule required. Remove "halted training until the benchmark was shown to be sound" from the abstract (the benchmark was never shown sound by the rule's own criterion). ## Section C. Paper 03 (ESE), evidence check of the results and the incident table

Sources: `article/scripts/extract-evidence.mjs`, `article/evidence/results.json`, `article/audit/claim-ledger.md`, `article/audit/baseline-audit.md`, `git show 0cdc4588c:astra_review.md`, commits e230b2146, 1ddbb0640, b8be865d5, d7a752d5e, 790b56e71, 544147d4d, d2357a0a6, 9f92b5c4f, f1a51479c, 03089bdec, f2dacb603; `evaluation/registry/{diag-009,diag-pairs-010,diag-pairs-fixed}`, `evaluation/registry/disk-guard.log`, `training/checkpoints/exp-016-wires/overnight.log`.

### C1. REJECT-level for 03's central claim: the "7,050 labels reproduced" check does not show what the paper says it shows
- Abstract: "every recorded evaluation label could be reproduced exactly from the saved outputs." 4.1: "The final audit recomputed the outcome class of every held-out item in ten late-programme evaluation runs, 7,050 records in all, from the saved generated programs, and every recomputed label agreed with the recorded one. Every program in those runs parsed and passed dependency-graph validation." 5.1: "the final audit found no disagreement between recorded and recomputed labels across thousands of records."
- What the audit code does (`article/scripts/extract-evidence.mjs` lines 96-101): it asserts that class totals in `holdout.jsonl` equal `metrics.json` (two files written by the same harness from the same run), and it re-applies `teacher/naming.mjs answerMatches(oracle, answer)` to rows already labelled `answer_match` or `answer_mismatch`. It never re-parses, re-validates, or re-executes any generated program. The rows actually re-compared number 5,592 of 7,050 (the sum of match plus mismatch across the ten arms in `results.json`); the 1,458 execution errors were not re-derived. "Every program parsed and passed graph validation" comes from the recorded class field (`parse_invalid: 0`, `graph_invalid: 0`), not from a re-parse.
- So the evidence establishes that the string comparator is deterministic and unchanged since the runs, not that "the computations were reliable". Re-applying the same function that produced the labels is close to tautological. The paper's organising asymmetry ("agents write reliable code and unreliable claims", 5.1) rests on this check.
- Fix (cheap, no inference): re-run all 7,050 saved completions through the parser, graph validator, and `runtime/kernel.mjs`, re-derive every class, and report agreement; until then, rewrite as "re-applying the answer comparator to the 5,592 completed answers reproduced every match/mismatch label; recorded class totals agree with the run metrics". Drop "from the saved generated programs". Tone down "the pipeline was sound" and the 5.1 heading.

### C2. MAJOR: "None of the consequential errors was a computational bug in the usual sense" is contradicted by 03's own table
- Abstract and 4.2 ("Only two were failures of the computing environment ... the remainder were cases in which an artifact was read as meaning something it did not").
- Table 1 includes plain code defects: "Retry attempts after the first never executed their programs" (1ddbb0640 calls it "the diagnostic retry-loop bug"); "The probe scorer accepted an answer if its first number matched" (astra_review R9: "compiled probe scoring can accept a wrong result"; fixed in 3bdb0eb07 "the compiled comparator stops accepting a wrong result"); "The evaluation server's readiness check did not confirm which checkpoint it was serving" (R2); demonstration selection reading the hidden reference (R1, a code defect). Relabelling each as a "seam" after the fact makes the seam taxonomy unfalsifiable: any bug can be described as an artifact read as meaning something it did not.
- Fix: state an operational criterion that separates a "computation bug" (code violates its own specification or docstring) from a "seam error" (code meets its specification but the specification was the wrong proxy), classify each incident with it, and report the split honestly (on my reading at least 4 of 17 are ordinary bugs). Soften the abstract accordingly.

### C3. MAJOR: the comparator problem was found by item reading about 15 hours before the solver test, and one of the "four experiments" was a response to it
- 03 Section 1: "four training experiments aimed at raising their scores had been aimed, in part, at the comparator." 4.4: "The team read the zeros as a learning failure and ran four experiments that restructured targets, changed the program idiom, and simplified statements. Only when a stop rule forced a check of the instrument did the solver test reveal that the comparator itself would fail correct work." 5.3: "Aggregates hid the comparator problem for eight days; a single scheduling item ... showed it immediately."
- Commit e230b2146 (26 Sep 03:55), "dv11: phrase compression - the two zero books print value-bearing answers": "render() strings shrink to the value tokens (e.g. '46 minutes: not feasible') ... the 14 changed families ship the computed answer as the oracle ... The measured evidence: 38 of 64 executed-but-wrong decompose rows had the right number and only paraphrased the verdict wording." That is an item-level diagnosis of the phrasing problem, and dv11 (exp-024) was the intervention on it. 03's list of the four experiments silently drops "compressed the reference answers" (04 includes it). The solver test's two passes were exactly the families dv11 had compressed (agent-eval report).
- Consequences: the "about 8 days" lag and the "found only when a stop rule forced a check" story are wrong; the correct story is that items revealed the phrasing problem on 26 Sep early morning, the team changed training targets and some references rather than the comparator, and the solver test 15 hours later showed that fix was insufficient. This also contradicts 4.7 ("Errors found by reading items were found within minutes to hours"), since the comparator row is labelled "items" with an 8-day lag.
- Fix: add the dv11 finding to 4.4 and Table 1 (detected by items, 26 Sep 03:55; response: compress targets; second detection: solver test 19:02). Replace "Halfway through our programme" (Section 1) - the solver test was on 26 Sep, day 9 of the 18-27 September window.

### C4. MAJOR: "None was in place before its incident" is false for the disk guard, and the guard's failure is itself evidence against Table 2's promise
- 4.8: "the disk guard after the lost night ... None was in place before its incident." Table 2 "Environment: ... guard that stops writers before the disk is full" is listed as a check that "would have caught" disk exhaustion.

The guard was strengthened afterwards (544147d4d, 24 Sep 07:45: 40/16 GiB thresholds, also stops downloads).
- So the mechanical check existed and did not prevent the incident (its threshold was below one checkpoint save and it did not stop downloads).
