# Fact-check: larger models (Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Sonnet 5) and judge statistics

Method: every number was recomputed from `evaluation/registry/m-{haiku,sonnet}-*/items.jsonl` (exact = `class === 'answer_match'`; semantic = exact or both judges "same", jids from `judge-A.jsonl`/`judge-B.jsonl`), from the per-batch judge files `judge-{A,B,pairs}-{0..6}.jsonl`, from the raw agent answers in `evaluation/registry/agent-models/` and `superseded/`, and from `article/experiments/build-agent-prompts.mjs`. The recomputed tables match `model-comparison.md` cell for cell.

## Findings (false or overstated)

### F1. "One condition per session" is false for 4 of the 9 accepted batches per model (HIGH)
- Locations: ML 9.4 ("Batches that mixed two conditions of the same problem ... were discarded and re-run with one condition per session"); Informatica after Table 2 ("received the same messages through agent sessions, one condition per session"); ESE 4.6 ("The affected batches were discarded and re-run with one condition per session"); claim-ledger last section ("one condition per session", "Batches redesigned (one condition per session)"); `article/evidence/comparison/README.md` ("Each session answered one condition of one set, so it never saw two wordings or two conditions of the same problem").
- Records: `build-agent-prompts.mjs` defines the accepted batches as `c1: e8-l0 ops + e8-l0 js`, `c2: e8-l2 ops + e8-l2 js`, `c3: e8-l0 sop + e9 js`, `c4: e8-l2 sop + e9 sop`, `c5: e3-original js + e3-original sop`, `c6: e3-procedural js + e3-procedural sop`; only `d1`, `d2`, `b2` hold one condition. The answer files confirm it (e.g. `answers-haiku-c1.jsonl` = 45 `e8-l0|ops` + 45 `e8-l0|js` keys on the same 45 problems; `answers-haiku-c5.jsonl` = 30 `e3-original|js` + 30 `e3-original|sop` on the same 30 problems). The builder's own comment claims only "at most one wording of any problem", which is true; the manuscripts turned it into "one condition".
- So c1, c2, c5, c6 put two conditions of the SAME problems in one session: the Informatica Table 2 operator-plan and JavaScript cells for both larger models, and the E3 book-scheduling/coalition cells (JS and SOP Lang) behind "solved all 15 book scheduling problems in the book's wording".
- Evidence of carry-over within these batches: in c5 and c6 the final answer string of the JavaScript and the SOP Lang reply is identical for 30 of 30 problems, for both models and both wordings (e.g. Haiku JS returns `criticalPath + " minutes. " + (meetsLimit ? "Yes, the plan meets the limit." : ...)` and the SOP Lang reply to the same problem returns the same phrase). Superseded batch b1 (main direct + main js, two conditions of the same problems, one wording) was discarded for exactly this kind of mixing, so the accepted design is inconsistent with the stated rule. The correctness of the E3 answers is not in doubt (see verified list), but the independence claim is.

### F2. The larger-model semantic scores rest on a judging pass with no calibration items (MEDIUM-HIGH)
- Locations: ML Section 2.3 and 9.4 ("scored with the same comparators and judges"); ESE 4.6; contribution sheet ("two blind judges (κ = 0.96), with calibration items"); README "Judge performance".
- Records: the 20 injected known-wrong pairs are all in batches 0-3 (6, 5, 5 and 5 calibration-key items, 21 in all, 20 known-wrong plus 1 known-same) and the 50 parser-equivalent pairs are in batches 0-4 (11, 13, 5, 12, 9). Batches 5 and 6 (the third pass, 538 pairs, judge files timestamped 06:06 on 29 September) contain only larger-model answers (269 + 269 of 269 + 269 pairs come from `m-*` records), with 0 known-wrong and 0 parser-equivalent items. 538 of the 619 distinct larger-model answer pairs were judged there; judge A said "same" to 499 of 538 (92.8%).
- The larger models' exact scores are low, so their reported numbers are almost entirely judge verdicts: e.g. Haiku main JS compositions 55 semantic vs 5 exact, book 52 vs 1; Sonnet main direct book 53 vs 0; all E3 cells 15 vs 0 exact; all E9 cells 40 vs 0 exact.
- The pooled statistics (κ = 0.96, 0 of 20 accepted) are correct but describe mainly the small-model passes. On the larger-model pairs alone, agreement is 99.5% (616 of 619), κ = 0.958 (recomputed), but no calibration was run on them. "Model and condition hidden, items shuffled" is also weaker in pass 3: every item in those two batches came from the two larger models. My deterministic re-checks (below) found the E3 scheduling and E9 verdicts correct, so this is a disclosure issue, not an error in those numbers.

### F3. "The results were identical across wordings ... which is how the defect showed. The agents had carried answers from one wording to the other" is not diagnostic as stated (MEDIUM)
- Location: ESE 4.6 "Mixed conditions in one agent batch"; claim-ledger ("gave identical answers across them"); README.
- Records supporting the claim: in superseded b3 the JavaScript replies of both models were byte-identical between the generator (L0) and narrative (L2) wording for 45 of 45 problems, and exact counts matched per wording (Haiku JS 36/36, ops 45/45; Sonnet JS 6/6, ops 45/45; superseded b4 SOP Lang Haiku 9/9, Sonnet 36/36).
- But the accepted re-run, where L0 and L2 were in separate sessions (c1 vs c2, c3 vs c4), shows the same pattern: Sonnet JS answers identical across wordings for 45 of 45 with exact 3 and 3; Haiku JS 42 of 45 identical with exact 6 and 6; raw JS replies byte-identical for 33 of 45 in both models, ops replies 45 of 45. Identity across wordings therefore also occurs without carry-over; the records show the design flaw but do not prove carry-over. Also note the ESE heading says "Mixed conditions" while the text describes mixed wordings (b3/b4/b5 mixed wordings and forms; b1 mixed conditions).

### F4. The "scripted batch" and the tool-use audit are not supported by retained records (MEDIUM)
- Locations: ML 9.4 ("We audited every session's tool use ... one batch whose replies were assembled by a script"); ESE 4.6 ("An audit of every session's tool use found one session..."); claim-ledger; README ("Sessions were not allowed to run scripts or code. Their tool use was audited.").
- Records: the only trace is the filename `superseded/answers-sonnet-b4.scripted.jsonl.bak` (130 replies). Its content is not distinguishable from accepted batches: 17 distinct reply skeletons (numbers and slot literals masked) against 16 in the non-scripted re-do `superseded/answers-sonnet-b4.jsonl` and 16 in the accepted `answers-sonnet-c3.jsonl`. No session transcripts, tool-use logs, audit results, or the instructions given to the agent sessions ("not to execute code or read other files") exist in the repository. This conflicts with ESE's and AI & Ethics' own emphasis on retained evidence. The claim may be true; it cannot be checked from the records.

### F5. "SOP Lang ... was as accurate as plain JavaScript for both larger models" (LOW-MEDIUM)
- Locations: Informatica design consequence 1; claim-ledger ("55 of 55 compositions each").
- Records: true for the 55 compositions and for Sonnet generally, but Haiku's SOP Lang was lower than its JavaScript on book problems (47 vs 52 of 55) and in generator wording (31 vs 33 of 33 held-out compositions, Table 2 itself). Informatica's Table 2 shows the 31 vs 33 directly under the sentence. "Nearly as accurate" or "as accurate on compositions" would be exact.

### F6. "for the small models it was the weakest form" (LOW)
- Location: Informatica design consequence 1.
- Records (Table 2): true at 1.5B and 1.7B in both wordings (the earlier paragraph correctly restricts it). At 0.5B in narrative wording SOP Lang (10 of 33) beat the operator plan (6 of 33). The unqualified restatement drops the "at 1.5B and 1.7B" condition.

### F7. "Code helped the smaller of the two larger models" (LOW)
- Location: ML 9.4, claim-ledger.
- The numbers (Haiku direct 48 and 41, JavaScript 55 and 52; Sonnet direct 55 and 53, JavaScript 55 and 54) are correct. "Smaller" is an assumption: parameter counts of Haiku 4.5 and Sonnet 5 are not published; "the weaker of the two" or "Haiku" is safer.

### F8. "the same problems, messages, and examples as the untuned small models" (LOW, already caveated)
- Location: ML 9.4, Informatica ("received the same messages").
- Records: the item sets are identical (checked: main 115, E8 45 per rung, E3 30, E9 40 match the small-model directories item for item and oracle for oracle), and the templates come from the same `messagesFor`. But the agents received a batch file (`prompts-*.json`: one template plus many statements) inside an agent session, not the per-item chat messages; in c1/c2/c5/c6 they also saw two templates. The caveat "a batched agent session is not identical to an independent API call" covers part of this; "same messages" is stronger than the records support.

## Claims verified as correct
- Main comparison, semantic (ML Table 7, contribution sheet, model-comparison.md): Haiku direct 48/55 compositions, 41/55 book; JS 55, 52; SOP 55, 47. Sonnet direct 55, 53; JS 55, 54; SOP 55, 53. Withheld-in-main: Haiku direct 0/5, all others 5/5.
- Withheld type (E9, all 40): 40/40 for both models in JS and SOP Lang. Independently re-verified by parsing days and ordering decision in all 160 answers: 0 errors (exact = 0/40 everywhere; all acceptances are judge verdicts, and correct).
- Rewording/abstraction (Table 7 last column, Informatica Table 2 semantic and exact-in-parentheses): Haiku ops 33 (33)/33 (33), JS 33 (6)/33 (6), SOP 31 (28)/33 (6); Sonnet ops 33 (33)/33 (33), JS 33 (3)/33 (3), SOP 33 (3)/33 (3). 33 held-out items per cell; same 45 items (33 held-out + 12 controls) as the small models and as E2. All nine small-model rows of Informatica Table 2, semantic and exact, also match.
- Small-model comparators in Table 7: 1.7B untuned JS 45/10/24 and 26/26; 1.7B fine-tuned compact 55/32/2 and 33/24 (E2 paired on the same 45 items as E8).
- "Solved all 15 book scheduling problems in the book's own wording" (both models, JS and SOP Lang): true semantically; independently verified by parsing minutes and feasibility in all 120 E3 scheduling answers, 0 errors (exact 0/15 in every cell; strict value 7/15 Haiku, 0/15 Sonnet in book wording). "which the final-data fine-tuned model never solved": exp027 0/15 in book wording.
- "Unaffected by rewording" / "narrative wording ... did not affect them": no larger-model cell drops from generator to narrative wording (Haiku SOP rises 31 to 33).
- "Operator plans gave exact answers every time" for the larger models; "JavaScript answers matched the reference wording in only 3 to 6 of 33".
- Contribution sheet: "55 of 55 ... JavaScript or SOP Lang, 47 to 54 of 55 book problems, all 40 withheld-type problems, 31 to 33 of 33 in both wordings; direct: Haiku 48 and 41, Sonnet 55 and 53".
- "Neither larger model gained accuracy from SOP Lang over plain JavaScript"; "Sonnet did not need it"; best fine-tuned 1.7B matched larger models on compositions (55/55).
- Judge statistics: 2,039 pairs = 2,019 distinct candidate answers + 20 injected known-wrong; observed agreement 98.2% (0.9823 over 2,039; 0.9822 over 2,019); κ = 0.965 (0.9646 over the 2,019), reported as 0.96; 36 disputed; 0 of 20 known-wrong accepted by either judge; 50 parser-equivalent pairs, all accepted by both; 7 batches x 2 judges = 14 sessions; three passes (files at 02:14, 03:51, 06:06 on 29 September); 0 missing verdicts.
- Superseded batches: b3 mixed L0 and L2 wordings (and ops+js), b4 mixed L0/L2 SOP Lang with E9 JS, b5 mixed E3 wordings with E9 SOP; b1 mixed direct and JS. In b3, JS replies were byte-identical across wordings 45/45 for both models and exact counts matched per wording.
- Spot check of judge-accepted, non-value-matching larger-model book answers (coalitions without seat counts, "Neither attempt succeeds", rule-scope explanations, percentage comparisons): verdicts are defensible; coalition answers omitting seat totals answer the question as posed ("Which coalitions are minimal winning coalitions?").
