## cfq: Measuring Compositional Generalization: A Comprehensive Method on Realistic Data

ICLR 2020 conference page (iclr.cc virtual poster SygcCnNKwr) and arXiv:1912.09713 metadata (comment: accepted at ICLR 2020), accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, venue and year checked.

Support: Train/test splits built to maximize compound divergence while keeping atom divergence small make three tested architectures fail to generalize compositionally, with a strong negative correlation between compound divergence and accuracy. Source: [primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09713), Abstract.

Boundary: Does not show that the same divergence measure governs a DSL-compilation task or small code-tuned decoder models; the result is for the three architectures tested in that paper.

## cogs: COGS: A Compositional Generalization Challenge Based on Semantic Interpretation

Crossref DOI record, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, venue and year checked.

Support: Transformers and LSTMs reach near-perfect in-distribution accuracy (96-99%) on COGS but much lower generalization accuracy (16-35%) on systematic gaps such as new combinations of familiar structures, with high seed sensitivity. Source: [primary source](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.731/), Abstract.

Boundary: Does not address pretrained code LLMs or fine-tuned small decoders; its generalization set mixes lexical and structural gaps, so it does not isolate structural generalization alone.

## hupkes: Compositionality Decomposed: How do Neural Networks Generalise?

Crossref DOI record, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, venue and year checked.

Support: Translates interpretations of compositionality into five task-independent tests: systematicity (recombining known parts and rules), productivity (extending beyond training length), localism, substitutivity (synonyms), and overgeneralisation (rules versus exceptions). Source: [primary source](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.11674), Abstract (Crossref-deposited).

Boundary: Does not supply a benchmark for program or circuit synthesis; the tests are instantiated on the artificial PCFG SET with recurrent, convolutional and transformer seq2seq models.

## faithfate: Faith and Fate: Limits of Transformers on Compositionality

Crossref DOI record and NeurIPS proceedings page, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, venue, volume, pages and year checked.

Support: On multi-digit multiplication, logic grid puzzles and a dynamic-programming task, transformer LLMs appear to solve compositional tasks by reducing multi-step reasoning to linearized subgraph matching rather than systematic problem solving, and performance can decay rapidly with increased task complexity. Source: [primary source](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/deb3c28192f979302c157cb653c15e90-Abstract-Conference.html), Abstract.

Boundary: Does not study DSL or circuit targets, and its decay argument is stated in terms of increased task complexity, not as a general law for every unseen composition.

## herzig: Unlocking Compositional Generalization in Pre-trained Models Using Intermediate Representations

arXiv abstract page for arXiv:2104.07478, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors and year checked; no peer-reviewed venue version found.

Support: Mapping natural language to a reversible or lossy intermediate representation with stronger structural correspondence to the input, instead of directly to the executable form, substantially improves compositional generalization of pretrained seq2seq models (e.g., +14.8 points on CFQ). Source: [primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07478), Abstract.

Boundary: Does not evaluate decoder-only code models or execution-verified DSL circuits; the evidence is from semantic parsing (CFQ and text-to-SQL template splits) and is a non-peer-reviewed preprint.

## bogin: Unobserved Local Structures Make Compositional Generalization Hard

Crossref DOI record, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, venue and year checked.

Support: Different models consistently fail on the same test instances, and a test instance is hard if it contains a local structure not observed at training time; this criterion predicts instance-level generalization across five semantic parsing datasets. Source: [primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05899), Abstract.

Boundary: Does not show that the local-structure criterion transfers to program/circuit generation outside semantic parsing.

## lora: LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models

ICLR 2022 conference page (iclr.cc virtual poster 6319) and arXiv:2106.09685 metadata, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, venue and year checked.

Support: LoRA freezes pretrained weights and trains low-rank decomposition matrices injected into Transformer layers, cutting trainable parameters greatly while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning quality on RoBERTa, DeBERTa, GPT-2 and GPT-3, with no added inference latency. Source: [primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685), Abstract.

Boundary: Does not establish that LoRA matches full fine-tuning for every target domain or for learning a new formal language; later work (Biderman et al., 2024) reports gaps on code and math.

## loraforgets: LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less

arXiv:2405.09673 final-version metadata (accepted to TMLR, August 2024, Featured Certification) and TMLR OpenReview forum id aloEru2qCG via the authors' repository, accessed 2026-09-28; the OpenReview page itself was behind a browser challenge.

Support: On programming and mathematics, in both instruction fine-tuning and continued pretraining, standard low-rank LoRA substantially underperforms full fine-tuning on the target domain but better maintains the base model's performance on tasks outside it, mitigating forgetting more than weight decay or dropout. Boundary: Does not test DSL-compilation targets or the small models used here; the target domains are programming and mathematics.

## specializing: Specializing Smaller Language Models towards Multi-Step Reasoning

PMLR volume 202 (ICML 2023) page and abstract, accessed 2026-09-28; title, authors, pages and year checked.

Support: Research misconduct is fabrication, falsification or plagiarism in proposing, performing or reviewing research, or in reporting research results, and does not include honest error or differences of opinion; a finding requires a significant departure from accepted practices of the relevant research community, commission intentionally, knowingly or recklessly, and proof by a preponderance of evidence. Source: [primary source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2000-12-06/html/00-30852.htm), Federal Policy on Research Misconduct, Sections I and II (printed pages 76262-76263).

Boundary: Is a United States federal policy, not a universal definition; says nothing about AI tools.

## allea: The European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity – Revised Edition 2023

The code's PDF (published in Berlin by ALLEA, June 2023; its own citation line gives 'ALLEA (2023) The European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity – Revised Edition 2023. Berlin. DOI 10.26356/ECOC', ISBN 978-3-9823562-3-5), accessed 2026-09-29; the DOI resolves (HTTP 302) to the ALLEA page for the 2023 code.

Support: Researchers report their results and methods, including the use of external services or AI and automated tools, in a way compatible with the norms of the discipline and facilitating verification (2.3); authorship includes agreeing to be responsible for the content of the publication (2.7); hiding the use of AI or automated tools in the creation of content or drafting of publications is listed among unacceptable practices (3.1). Source: [primary source](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf), Section 2.3 (Research Procedures); Section 2.7 (Publication, Dissemination, and Authorship); Section 3.1 (Research Misconduct and other Unacceptable Practices).

Boundary: Does not require per-record provenance of agent-produced research records; that duty is the authors' proposal.
