## Machine Learning

Dear Editors,

Please consider “Choosing the language a small model must learn: executable abstractions in program compilation” as a research article. The paper studies the executable target language as an experimental variable. Adding specialized commands accompanies an increase in normalized exact matching from 53.8% to 62.4%; splitting computations into more wires instead raises execution failures from 8.1% to 19.1%. The interpretation is that removing algorithm construction and adding component interfaces place different demands on a small model.

The paper also reconstructs archived comparisons with the released instruction-tuned bases. It applies common scorers to paired outputs and states their weaknesses, the different generation budgets, and the confounding of adaptation with execution. These comparisons contextualize the complete workflow without claiming an isolated causal benefit from the interpreter.

The contribution is a bounded representation study and a prospective design for discovering useful commands. One run per condition, adaptive benchmark use, and incomplete early metadata are explicit limitations. A contribution information sheet and reconstruction package accompany the manuscript. Coding-agent assistance is disclosed.

## Informatica

Dear Editor,

Please consider “SOP Lang: Moving Algorithms into Explicit Commands for Small-Model Compilation” as a technical paper in the Normal processing track, N. The contribution is a working target language that exposes restricted operations alongside generated JavaScript. A complete syntax example explains declarations, bodies, dependencies, and execution. An executable graph example then shows what a command takes over and what interpretation remains the model's responsibility.

Two design comparisons motivate the language boundary: specialized commands improve recorded performance, whereas additional target decomposition reduces execution reliability. The paper separates those observations from the proposed mechanism and describes a concrete admission procedure for future commands. Its diagrams explain notation and the distinction between directed and undirected reachability.

The five-page source uses the official Informatica template styles and geometry, with a rendered PDF available. AI-assisted implementation, analysis, and writing are disclosed. Actual author details, the short Slovenian abstract, and the final public data-access record remain to be completed before submission.

## Empirical Software Engineering

Dear Editors,

Please consider “When correct metrics support the wrong claim: auditing agent-assisted research software” as an exploratory research case study. Reconstructing saved outcome labels verifies the metrics while exposing different problems in their interpretation: model identity, population continuity, scoring comparability, missing judgments, and unsupported attribution of a mechanism.

The contribution is a claim-centered audit whose operations can be applied to other experimental repositories. A new reconstruction of archived base-model evaluations illustrates the difference between applying a common scorer and obtaining an unbiased capability comparison. A constructed directed-graph counterexample shows why agreement between implementations need not provide independent evidence about the original problem.

The study preserves the genuine positive abstraction result. It makes no agent-versus-human error-rate or productivity claim. Research questions, evidence states, reflexivity, and validity limitations are explicit, and the accompanying analysis scripts and mappings support reconstruction.

## Open Research Europe

Dear Editorial Team,

Please consider “What executable answers reveal and hide: reusable evidence for evaluating small-model compilation” as a research article, once author eligibility and public-deposit requirements have been established. The paper examines how the interpretation of a model comparison changes when structural validity, execution, task family, and answer equivalence are reported separately.

On a common evaluation set, the adapted 1.7B system improves overall exact matching from 51.2% to 59.7% relative to the adapted 0.5B system, but parallel-task scheduling becomes less reliable: execution failures rise from 55% to 94%. Separate retrospective diagnostics identify answer-format penalties while retaining disagreements, unsupported prose, and execution failures. These findings distinguish opportunities for new commands from problems that call for a better answer comparator.

The contribution is a reproducible evaluation analysis with item-level decisions and a prospective research design. The manuscript distinguishes archived measurements, retrospective diagnostics, and future experiments, and describes coding-agent assistance and the limits of synthetic-data checks. The submission must identify the actual eligibility route and provide the permitted public evidence deposit; neither an EU grant nor existing public availability is assumed here.

## AI and Ethics

Dear Editors,

Please consider “Who is responsible for an executable claim? Epistemic accountability in agent-assisted research” as a research article. The paper asks what makes computational delegation a justified source of scientific evidence when agents also help construct the training examples, checks, and interpretation.

A concrete small-model compilation study grounds the argument. The paper recognizes a real benefit from specialized operations while showing how correct execution and reproducible metrics can coexist with mistaken task interpretation or unsupported scientific claims. It develops four duties: describe the property actually checked, preserve the evidence needed to disagree, attach uncertainty to the inference it changes, and retain human responsibility for publication.

The case does not supply a human-team comparison or a measured social-impact result. The review manuscript omits identifying author metadata and repository links. An anonymized evidence supplement and the actual author/declaration fields should accompany submission. AI assistance is disclosed as substantive participation in research tooling and writing.
