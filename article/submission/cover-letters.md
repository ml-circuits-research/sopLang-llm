## Machine Learning

Dear Editors,

Please consider “Learning to select executable abstractions: evidence and limits from small language models compiling word problems” as a research article. The paper examines target vocabulary as an experimental variable in small-model compilation. Reconstructed item records show a gain from 379/705 to 440/705 normalized exact matches after specialized commands are introduced, together with a counterexample in which additional target decomposition raises execution failures. The analysis distinguishes structural validity, execution, task interpretation, and restricted answer-equivalence diagnostics.

The contribution is a bounded empirical study and a concrete protocol for future vocabulary discovery. We state the single-run design, adaptive benchmark use, and missing early training metadata explicitly. The manuscript is accompanied by the required contribution information sheet and an evidence reconstruction package. Coding-agent assistance is disclosed in the paper.

## Empirical Software Engineering

Dear Editors,

Please consider “From experiment logs to defensible claims: an artifact audit of coding-agent-assisted small-model research” as an exploratory research case study. The study examines the chain from experimental artifacts to scientific claims in one research-software repository. Reconstructing 7,050 item records reproduces the original numerical outcomes while exposing separate problems in model identity, population continuity, semantic-judgment traceability, and mechanism attribution.

The paper's contribution is a claim-centered audit with executable operations and explicit evidence states. It preserves a substantive positive abstraction result and a negative decomposition result instead of treating auditing as a reason to discard the experiment. It makes no agent-versus-human productivity claim. The case boundary, research questions, methods, reflexivity, and validity limitations are stated, and the accompanying package supports reconstruction.

## AI and Ethics

Dear Editors,

Please consider “Executable answers and the limits of delegated understanding: a case for epistemic accountability in agent-assisted research” as a research article. The paper asks what makes computational delegation a justified source of evidence when agents also help construct the checks and interpretation. It develops duties of accurate representation to readers, local specification of verification claims, disclosure of shared assumptions, and preservation of uncertainty and negative evidence.

A concrete experimental case grounds the normative argument. The paper recognizes a real benefit from executable abstractions while examining how internal consistency can coexist with unsupported interpretations. It does not infer social-deployment safety or compare agents with a human-only team. The review manuscript omits identifying author metadata and repository links; an anonymized evidence supplement and the required author/declaration fields should accompany submission. AI assistance is disclosed substantively rather than characterized as copy editing alone.
