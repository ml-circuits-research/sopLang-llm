# 3. Hypotheses

Five hypotheses are measured, not asserted. Each is one paragraph: the claim, the evidence, and the number that supports or weakens it.

## H-coverage — the model learns what it is shown

The model answers what it has been shown, and almost nothing else: roughly 98–99% on plan shapes that occur in training, and 12–25% on shapes that occur nowhere. The evidence is the plan-seen/plan-unseen ladder, stable across every arm of the series. On the validation slice the 323 rows whose plan fingerprint occurs in the training rows score 98.1–98.8% at the winner checkpoint of every full-fine-tuning arm, while the 16 rows whose plan occurs nowhere score 12.5–25.0% (2 to 4 of 16), no matter the mixture, the recipe, the target length, or the phrasing. The untrained base parses 0 of 50 holdout statements; after fine-tuning, parse and graph validity are 100.0% at essentially every checkpoint — so the model learned the *language*, and the residual failure is purely whether it has a procedure for the plan the statement needs. The lever is coverage of plan shapes, not volume of the same shapes: more instances of a covered plan buy recall, not generalization.

## H-vocabulary — composition generalizes inside a trained operator vocabulary, not across it

Chains of trained operators generalize at ~100% on unseen chains, while an unseen *operation* drops to first-exposure levels. The census arm (`exp-012-census`) attacked the coverage boundary directly by declaring an inventory of operator chains and holding out whole compositions before rendering — four chains at 40 rows each, 160 held-out rows in the first census. The trained composition clusters score 40/40 on held-out instances; a held-out composition — a chain the trainer never showed, even though every operator in it is familiar — drops sharply (0 to 9 of 40, against 40/40 for the trained chains); and the book families, whose operations sit entirely outside the taught vocabulary, barely move: the seven book holdout rows never rose above a handful of correct answers out of 225 across the whole series, while the arithmetic book — built from taught operators — reached 71.1–91.5%. Scale did not move this boundary: the 1.7B arm (442/705) and the 1.7B arm (436/705, 442/705) land on the same ~62.7% ceiling, so scale is a multiplier of proven data, not a substitute for it.

## H-wires — declarative wire types absorb recurring multi-line transcriptions and improve the outcome

The hypothesis behind `graphPath`, `aggregate`, and `fraction`: when a recurring multi-line JavaScript transcription (an adjacency-map BFS, a filter-then-reduce pair, a greatest-common-divisor loop) moves out of the model's body and becomes a named command whose contract the runtime enforces, the class of transcription errors becomes *impossible* rather than merely rarer. The measured arm is `exp-016-wires` against `exp-014-deep-chains`: same base, same data content, the only change being that the deep-chain transcriptions are emitted as declarative wire commands. The holdout oracle match rose from 379/705 to 440/705, and procedural-arithmetic execution errors fell from 63 to 13 — the model stopped emitting the free-form list and graph code that produces "is not iterable" and "is not a function" failures, because that code no longer exists to be mis-emitted. The one-operation chains (division, modulo, unique-count) were explicitly *not* promoted: a wire that only renames a one-line expression reduces no code, and the proposal discipline rejects it as prettification.

## H-containers — typed persistent state replaces hand-rolled lists

The book families' longest bodies are not arithmetic; they are stateful bookkeeping — schedules, coalitions, evidence trees — that hand-roll lists, membership checks, and merge logic inside a 40-to-67-line `jsEval` body. The hypothesis is that a typed persistent container (`container` declares a schema and identity policy; `containerAdd`/`containerUpsert`/`containerRemove` stage patches; `containerFilter` derives a provenance-keeping view) replaces that hand-rolled state, so the model names a store instead of transcribing list surgery. The runtime already ships the five container commands; the four coupled gates that blocked them from the data pipeline (the family validator's whitelist, the program builder's missing container path, the provenance battery's reactivity judgment, and the missing manifest column) are documented in decision D-G and, at the time of writing, steps 1–3 have landed, step 4 is deferred, the three minimal container families are in progress, and the measurement arm is queued. ## H-structure — modular multi-wire targets against monolithic bodies (the bloat hypothesis)

The series prioritized coverage, then declarative wires, then containers — and deliberately deferred restructuring the book families' monolithic compute bodies, because doing it safely required the declarative commands and container gates that only exist now. The static data-quality checker quantifies the deferred debt as a **bloat indicator**: on the current suite, 2.19 wires per plan against 15.4 `jsEval` lines per plan (7.0 lines per wire), with 1,468 plans (13.8%) carrying at most three wires but more than 25 `jsEval` lines — too few wires for the JavaScript mass. The hypothesis is that holding statements, oracles, and printed answers identical while refactoring those flagged bodies into modular multi-wire plans changes the student's measured quality. The experiment is designed as a controlled pair: `exp-021` (dv7, monolithic) against `exp-022` (dv8, modular), same base, same recipe, same row counts, with the verify gate proving every refactored circuit reproduces its printed answer and reacts to its inputs.
