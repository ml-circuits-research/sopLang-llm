# 5. Discussion

## What is new

Five things are new here, and none of them is a model-size claim.

**The abstraction-learning loop.** The series runs a standing cycle of *measure, flag, propose, validate, measure again*. The wire-discovery tool ranks every recurring `jsEval` shape in the shipped suite by (frequency × mean-lines × error-share); the data-quality checker flags the monstrous bodies; a wire is proposed with its contract and its measured line reduction and error elimination; it is adopted only after the family round-trip/oracle tests pass and `node training-data/verify.mjs` reproduces every printed answer; and the next arm's holdout measures the change against the `jsEval` baseline. Only a measured win keeps the wire. That discipline is why `graphPath` shipped and the one-line operations (division, modulo, unique-count) did not — the loop rejects prettification the same way it promotes a real transcription removal. The loop is also what makes the modularity null publishable: the same cycle measured dv8, found the change costs, and said so.

**The declarative wire types.** `graphPath`, `aggregate`, and `fraction` are the concrete result of the loop. They move a recurring multi-line transcription out of the model's body and into a command whose contract the runtime enforces, which converts a class of mis-emission errors into an impossibility. The measured effect — 379/705 to 440/705, procedural execution errors 63 to 13 — is a *data* result, not a recipe result: the same base, the same content, only the target structure changed.

**The container abstraction, and its measured boundary.** Typed persistent state (`container` plus its staged patches and derived views) is wired into the generator as a first-class shape, so the model learns to name a store instead of hand-rolling lists. Its measured effect is the paper's headline: world-as-a-system 20/20 and decompose-to-solve 0 → 66/100 semantic, while the 17B stays at zero on both books. Its boundary is equally measured: the container idiom did *not* transfer to the units-and-rates and dependency-chain shapes (dv9 left both books at zero and cost 28 procedural and 4 world answers), so the abstraction is real but shape-specific, not a general compiler faculty.

**The semantic re-scorer.** The original exact-phrase comparison understated every model. The re-score — a value-based judge that accepts an answer stating the same numbers and the same verdict, wording ignored — is itself a measured artifact: 2,264 mismatched rows re-judged, 1,431 rejected for a missing oracle number, 833 judged by four independent subagents, 440 accepted. It turned decompose-to-solve from a zero into the container arm's 66/100, and it is the recommendation for the benchmark going forward.

**The static data-quality checker with the bloat indicator.** One number — wires per plan against `jsEval` lines per plan — turns a qualitative sense of "these bodies are too big" into a measurable, re-checkable quantity (2.19 wires vs 15.4 lines, 1,468 plans in the bloat class on dv7). The structure experiment is that indicator turned into a controlled pair, and the pair returned a null: structure is real, but gratuitous splitting doubles execution errors (57 → 135).

## The benchmark finding: the comparator was the bottleneck

The exact-phrase scorer is the single most consequential measurement of the series, because it retroactively changes how every earlier zero is read. A competent writer computed ~18 of 20 holdout items of the two stuck books but scored 2 of 20: the recorded answers carry unit words, punctuation, and prose wrappers that no solver can derive from the statement. The books were not measuring whether the model *computed* the right answer; they were measuring whether it reproduced the oracle's *phrasing*. Under semantic scoring, decompose-to-solve is not zero (66/100 for the container arm), and only common-sense remains genuinely unsolved. The lesson generalizes beyond this project: a phrase-matcher over free-form answers silently converts a computation benchmark into a paraphrase-reproduction benchmark, and the fix — value-based comparison with a rounding tolerance — is cheap and should be the default.

## The common-sense cause and the fix

All 50 common-sense holdout items are `units-and-rates` — a three-step fractional chain: rate = throughput × (100 − overhead%) / 100, quantity = rate × minutes / 60, both rounded to hundredths (round-half-to-even), then formatted. The books every arm solves well are integer slot arithmetic; the container abstraction transferred because it is symbolic, while units-and-rates is decimal-chain arithmetic. Two mechanisms explain the persistent zero:

1. The deep fractional-chain training data (dataVersion 2, the "deep tranche", 9,495 rows) was **removed** in dv7 and later. Only `exp-014-deep-chains` trained on it, and it is the only arm that computes units-and-rates — 49/50 semantic, with only float-format misses.
2. The mixed-book plan style teaches strict integer probes (e.g. scientific-reasoning form-27: `probe(Number.isInteger(rate) && rate > 0, ...)`). The model copies those probes onto the one fractional book, so roughly two-thirds of dv12's common-sense rows die as `execution_error` ("must be a whole positive quantity") before the value is ever compared.

The fix is fourfold and all data-level: restore a fractional-chain tranche to the SFT mix (target common-sense semantic ≥ 40/50, execution-error share < 10%); strip or relax the integer probes on non-common-sense plans (target zero "probe failed: … whole" errors); broaden the common-sense holdout with a per-shape breakdown so a fix on one family is visible; and adopt the value-based scorer with rounding tolerance. The repaired data is the input to the pending 0.5B run (`{{TODO: exp-026 0.5B}}`).

## What remains open

**The 0.5B comparison.** The original standing goal — a small model approaching 90% on the compiled benchmark — is dropped in favor of the comparative claim this paper makes: a small model trained through the loop beats a larger one on the reasoning books. The open question is whether the win is robust to a still smaller student: the 0.5B (which reached 362/705, 51.3% exact, on the pre-repair deep-chains data in `exp-015`) is being trained on the repaired data and compared against the 1.7B container arm. `{{TODO: exp-026 0.5B}}`

**The remaining zero books.** scientific-reasoning, adult-reasoning, and logical-reasoning stay near zero (0–8 of 25, or 0–5 of 10) across arms, semantically included. The vocabulary hypothesis reads these as the *boundary* of the trained operator vocabulary — graph traversal, probability, time arithmetic, and geometry are not yet in the census — and predicts they move only when the loop grows the vocabulary, not when the model grows.

## Operations as part of the method

Two nights were lost to the machinery, and they are part of the result, not a footnote. The night of 2026-09-23 died on a full disk: base-model downloads plus checkpoint saves filled the drive mid-save, corrupting a checkpoint and killing an arm at step 160 of 630 — and one closed arm alone (exp-013) had held 116 GiB of dead checkpoints. Twice before that, three trainers were launched at once and both nights were lost to concurrent corruption. The fixes are now policy, and they encode the lessons: one worker at a time, a preflight gate that refuses rather than warns, the completion signal as a result artifact rather than a log line (a failed chain also writes "done"), a disk guard that warns below 40 GiB and stops work below 16 GiB, and a watcher that *keeps waiting* on a stale failure line instead of exiting — because an exited watcher kills the queue silently, which is exactly what happened to exp-016's first chain. The honest reading: unattended training is itself an experiment in discipline, and the discipline is now part of the artifact.
