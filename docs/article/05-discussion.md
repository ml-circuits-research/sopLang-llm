# 5. Discussion

## What is new

Four things are new here, and none of them is a model-size claim.

**The abstraction-learning loop.** The series runs a standing cycle of *measure, flag, propose, validate, measure again*. The wire-discovery tool ranks every recurring `jsEval` shape in the shipped suite by (frequency × mean-lines × error-share); the data-quality checker flags the monstrous bodies; a wire is proposed with its contract and its measured line reduction and error elimination; it is adopted only after the family round-trip/oracle tests pass and `node training-data/verify.mjs` reproduces every printed answer; and the next arm's holdout measures the change against the `jsEval` baseline. Only a measured win keeps the wire. That discipline is why `graphPath` shipped and the one-line operations (division, modulo, unique-count) did not — the loop rejects prettification the same way it promotes a real transcription removal.

**The declarative wire types.** `graphPath`, `aggregate`, and `fraction` are the concrete result of the loop. They move a recurring multi-line transcription out of the model's body and into a command whose contract the runtime enforces, which converts a class of mis-emission errors into an impossibility. The measured effect — 379/705 to 440/705, procedural execution errors 63 to 13 — is a *data* result, not a recipe result: the same base, the same content, only the target structure changed.

**The container teaching pipeline.** Typed persistent state (`container` plus its staged patches and derived views) is wired into the generator as a first-class shape, so the model learns to name a store instead of hand-rolling lists. The four gates that blocked it are recorded as decision D-G, and the plan to land them is a dependency-ordered sequence whose verification runs in every phase — the same rigor as the wires, because a container that cannot be proven reactive is a template.

**The static data-quality checker with the bloat indicator.** One number — wires per plan against `jsEval` lines per plan — turns a qualitative sense of "these bodies are too big" into a measurable, re-checkable quantity (2.19 wires vs 15.4 lines, 1,468 plans in the bloat class). The structure experiment is that indicator turned into a controlled pair.

## What remains open

**The 90% benchmark hypothesis.** The standing goal is a small model that approaches 90% on the compiled benchmark, and the series is at 62.7% (442/705) — the ceiling reached independently by the 1.5B and the 1.7B arms. The failure is no longer syntactic and no longer "the program breaks": by dv5, runtime completion is 84.4% and procedural execution errors are down to a single row. The residual error is the model answering a *different question* than the one asked — the pattern-completion failure the contrastive and diagnostic arms identified: the model assembles the nearest memorized family rather than applying the operation the statement requests. The open question is whether the structure experiment (modular targets) and the container shapes move *that* number, or whether the benchmark's ceiling is a property of the coverage boundary itself. `{{TODO: whether modular targets move oracle match past 62.7%}}`.

**The book boundary.** The seven book families — reasoning about schedules, coalitions, evidence, minimum information — still score at or near zero on the holdout across the entire series, while the procedural-arithmetic families built from taught operators reach 91.5%. The vocabulary hypothesis says this is the *boundary* of the trained operator vocabulary, not a data-size problem, and the census plus the base shootout (1.7B ≈ 1.7B) both support that reading. Whether the next tranche of census operations (graph traversal, probability, time arithmetic, geometry) moves the book families is an open, measured question rather than an assumption.

## Operations as part of the method

Two nights were lost to the machinery, and they are part of the result, not a footnote. The night of 2026-09-23 died on a full disk: base-model downloads plus checkpoint saves filled the drive mid-save, corrupting a checkpoint and killing an arm at step 160 of 630 — and one closed arm alone (exp-013) had held 116 GiB of dead checkpoints. Twice before that, three trainers were launched at once and both nights were lost to concurrent corruption. The fixes are now policy, and they encode the lessons: one worker at a time, a preflight gate that refuses rather than warns, the completion signal as a result artifact rather than a log line (a failed chain also writes "done"), a disk guard that warns below 40 GiB and stops work below 16 GiB, and a watcher that *keeps waiting* on a stale failure line instead of exiting — because an exited watcher kills the queue silently, which is exactly what happened to exp-016's first chain. The honest reading: unattended training is itself an experiment in discipline, and the discipline is now part of the artifact.
